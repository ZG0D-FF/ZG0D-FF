🌟 Case Study: Telco Churn

An Interactive Story: "Which customers are likely to leave?"

1. The Problem

Customer churn has increased from 8% to 15%. Losing customers reduces revenue, and retaining customers is significantly cheaper than acquiring new ones. We need predictive analytics to identify flight-risk customers before they cancel.

15%

2. Risky Variables

  • Payment Delay: Customers paying late may be dissatisfied or financially stressed. (Higher delay = 100% churn rate).
  • Service Calls: Frequent complaints indicate poor service experience.
  • Lower Tenure: Customers with short contracts have less commitment.

3. AI Techniques Applied

Logistic Regression: Fits a Sigmoid curve to predict the binary outcome (Yes/No). Outputs a clear probability.

Decision Tree: Splits data by asking a series of questions (e.g., "Is the monthly bill high?").

Random Forest: Builds a whole forest. Combines many different models to improve results by taking a "majority vote".

4. Actions & Solutions

Marketing Strategy: Target only high-risk customers identified by the model (e.g., 200 customers). Avoid unnecessary cost by not giving discounts to loyal customers.

Operations Strategy: Improve service quality to reduce customer complaints. Resolve customer issues faster when service calls occur. Improve billing systems.

What is Google ADK?

Agent Development Kit (ADK) is a flexible and modular framework for developing and deploying AI agents. It enables complex multi-agent connections within maintainable workflows, offering a higher-level framework than standard LangChain or raw function calling.

Core Architecture Components

  • AI Model: The "Brain" that processes logic and decides actions.
  • Tools: The "Hands" that interact with APIs and databases.
  • Memory: The "Retention" storing state and history.
  • Workflows: The "Logic" defining collaboration.
  • Runner: Connects the Agent to the Session to generate responses.

The Agent-as-a-Tool Workflow

Building a team of specialists. The Top-Level Orchestrator evaluates prompt instructions and delegates tasks to Mid-Level Agents.

Mid-Level Agents can then decide to use their own tools (e.g., a Specialist Agent) to execute deep-dive analysis before returning the result back up the chain.

🤖 Part 1: Foundations of AI

Understanding Artificial Intelligence, its evolution, and the machine learning paradigms that drive it.

Artificial Intelligence

The overarching concept of creating intelligent machines that simulate human thinking and behavior.

Machine Learning

A subset of AI that allows machines to learn from data without being explicitly programmed.

Deep Learning

A subset of ML leveraging multiple layers of artificial neural networks for complex in-depth data processing.

The Evolution of AI

1956 - 1980: AI = Fast

Rule-Based Systems: Early AI focused on rapidly executing hardcoded rules. If temp=high and headache=yes, then output flu. It was fast, but strictly bounded by explicit programming.

1980 - 2000: AI = Learn

Machine Learning: Systems began finding mapping equations from data. Giving the model "I love Bangkok" outputs a positive sentiment without hardcoded rules.

2000 - 2015: AI = Perceive

Deep Learning: Multi-layered neural networks unlocked computer vision and advanced NLP, allowing AI to perceive and classify the world (e.g., identifying a dog in an image).

2015 - Now: AI = Create

Generative AI: AI transitions from prediction to synthesis. Trained on vast datasets, multimodal models can now generate human-like text, images, audio, and code.

📈 Part 2: Telecom Churn Case Study

Examining why customer churn has increased from 8% to 15% and how to predict which customers are likely to leave.

Dataset & Key Statistics

The Dataset

Includes features such as Customer Id, Age, Monthly Fee, Contract Length, Tenure, Service Calls, Payment Delay, Internet Type, and Churn (Yes/No).

Payment Delay Impact

Low delay (0-4 days) means 0% churn.
Higher delay (>4 days) means 100% churn. Late-paying customers are high risk.

Service Calls

Frequent complaints and support calls usually indicate poor service experience, significantly increasing churn risk.

AI & Predictive Models

Classification Approach

Predicting whether a customer will churn (Yes/No) based on historical data using techniques like Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, and Random Forest.

Feature Importance

Strongest impact: Payment delay days.
Increases churn: Many service calls.
Reduces churn: Longer contract length and longer tenure.
Moderate/Small effect: Monthly fee and Age.

Ethics & Risks

Biased training data causes unfair decisions. Model overfitting reduces performance on new information. Poor data quality causes unreliable analysis.

Business Decision Simulation

Campaign cost: $50 per customer

Customer value: $600 per year

Model Identifies: 200 high risk customers

Expected save rate: 40%

Strategic Action Plan:
  • Marketing: Target ONLY high-risk customers with retention discounts. Avoid unnecessary costs on loyal customers.
  • Operations: Improve service quality to reduce complaints. Resolve issues faster. Improve billing systems to reduce payment delays.

Group Discussion & Business Hypotheses

Business Hypothesis Rationale & Expected Pattern
Late payers are more likely to churn Customers who frequently delay payments may be less committed or financially stressed.
Frequent support contacts predict churn Indicates poor service experience or unresolved issues causing customer dissatisfaction.
New customers (low tenure) have higher churn Customers with short contracts have less commitment, so switching providers is easier.

Advanced Analytics: Risky Variables

Variable Why it looks risky Indicator to monitor
Payment delay days Signals financial stress or disengagement; often a sharp churn inflection after a small threshold Rising proportion of customers with >X days late
Service calls Frequent contacts usually mean unresolved problems and dissatisfaction High call counts and repeat tickets per customer
Tenure and contract length Short tenure and short contracts lower switching costs and commitment High churn in early months or at contract end
Plan price and monthly fee Perceived poor value increases price‑sensitive churn Churn spikes after price increases or for low‑value plans
Complaint severity & resolution time Slow or poor resolutions convert complaints into defections Long average time to close and low first‑call resolution
Usage decline Falling usage often precedes cancellation as customers disengage Month‑over‑month drop in minutes/data/feature use

Patterns to Expect

  • Threshold effects: Small delay increases have little effect until ~3-5 days.
  • Monotonic relationships: More calls and longer resolutions correlate with higher churn.
  • Protective tenure effect: Churn drops with tenure but spikes near renewals.
  • Interaction effects: Late payers with many service calls are exponentially riskier.
  • Segment differences: Price sensitivity and age create distinct behaviors.

Validation & Metrics

  • Model metrics: AUC, precision at top decile, lift for flagged customers.
  • Experiment metrics: Retention lift, incremental revenue, ROI.
  • Operational metrics: Time to resolution, first-call resolution rate.
  • Monitoring: Track model performance over time to detect data drift.

Quick Next Steps

  1. Confirm data quality for payment dates, call logs, and tenure.
  2. Run exploratory analysis to validate payment-delay thresholds and interaction effects.
  3. Prioritize two experiments: a retention offer A/B test and an SLA improvement pilot for high-call customers.

Experiment Hypothesis Testing

1. Late Payments Cause Churn

Test: Control for tenure/plan; run logistic regression with payment delay as categorical and continuous; check causal direction.

2. Unresolved Calls Drive Churn

Test: Compare churn for customers with many calls but fast resolution vs many calls with slow/no resolution.

3. New Customers & Onboarding

Test: Cohort analysis for first 3 months; pilot enhanced onboarding for a random subset.

4. Targeted Retention ROI

Test: A/B test incentives for top decile risk group; measure retention lift and lifetime value vs campaign cost.

Sequential Agent

Runs sub-agents one by one; used when Step B needs the result of Step A.

  • Shared State: The glue between agents.
  • output_key: Agent writes its output to this state.
  • {placeholder}: Next agent reads from state dynamically.
