Module 1: Engineering Economics

Eng. economics = analysing costs and benefits that occur at different times.

Use it when the problem is important, can't be solved in the head, and has an economic side.

Decision needs at least 2 alternatives and must be rational.

  • Market consequences: priced in the market (materials, labour)
  • Extra-market: shadow prices (cost of an injury)
  • Intangible: can't be priced (job loss from automation). Brought in at step 8.

9 steps of rational decision making

  1. Recognize the problem (TQM, continuous improvement)
  2. Define the goal. Conflicting goals = complex problem
  3. Assemble data. Direct cost traced, indirect (overhead) allocated
  4. Identify feasible alternatives. Never forget do-nothing
  5. Select criterion. Usually maximize profit
  6. Construct model (e.g. capacity = LW/K)
  7. Predict outcomes. Do-nothing does NOT mean no change
  8. Choose best. Drop dominated ones (walk 10 km vs bus)
  9. Audit results: actual vs predicted

Criterion (max profit)

Fixed input → maximize benefit
Fixed output → minimize cost
Neither fixed → maximize (benefit − cost)

Engineering costs

  • Fixed: no change with output (building, land)
  • Variable: changes with output (power, labour, material)
  • Total = fixed + variable
  • Marginal = ΔTC / Δoutput (cost of 1 more unit)
  • Average = TC / units (Rs 400 / 100 pens = Rs 4)
  • Sunk: already spent, IGNORE it
  • Opportunity: benefit given up by using a resource one way
  • Recurring (regular) vs non-recurring (irregular)
  • Incremental: extra cost of choosing one over another
  • Cash cost = real money moves. Book cost = only in books

Break-even

TC = F + Vx   TR = Px
xBE = F / (P − V)

Bus trip: F = 7,200 + 6,750 + 1,800 + 4,500 = 20,250.
V = 1,125 + 675 = 1,800 per person. P = 2,500.
x = 20,250 / 700 = 28.9, so 29 people.

Life-cycle cost

  • 6 phases: needs, concept design, detailed design, production, use, retirement
  • Later change = higher cost. Early decisions lock in later cost
  • Consider liability, testing, maintenance, warranty, disposal

Solved problems

Blend: 0.25x + 0.40(1−x) = 0.31, so x = 0.6. Cost = 0.6(270) + 0.4(396) = Rs 320.4/m3

Old pumps: ignore sunk costs, price on today's market (about Rs 2,70,000)

B minus A: +7,500 purchase, +1,500 install, −1,750/yr maintenance, +800/yr utility, −200 disposal

Liz is the decision maker (she picked the shortlist)

Machine part: switch. Saves Rs 11/pc direct on 15 lakh pcs vs Rs 1 lakh tooling

Module 2: Cash Flow and Rate of Return

  • CFD: time line, arrow down = cost, up = revenue. All flows at end of period
  • Categories: first cost, O&M, salvage, revenue, overhaul
  • Patterns: single, uniform, linear gradient, geometric gradient, irregular
  • Equivalence: Rs 300 @15% for 7 yrs ≡ Rs 798 (300 × 2.66). @10% ≡ Rs 585

Rates of return

  • MARR: lowest return that is acceptable
  • IRR: rate where NPW = 0. Accept if IRR ≥ MARR
  • ERR: return when reinvested at external rate (usually = MARR)
Σ CFt / (1+IRR)t = 0   (trial and error)
ERR = (FV / P)1/n − 1

IRR: invest 10,000, get 7,000 (yr 1) and 8,000 (yr 2). NPW +2,974 @10%, +118 @30%, +5 @31%, so IRR is about 31%.
ERR: FV = 6,000(1.1) + 8,000 = 14,600, ERR = (1.46)1/2 − 1 = 20.83%

Annual cash flow

Fixed input → max EUAB
Fixed output → min EUAC
Neither → max (EUAB − EUAC)
  • Salvage = inflow at end of life (after-tax if taxes)
  • Analysis period: common multiple of lives, continuing need, or infinite

Incremental ROR

  1. List all alternatives
  2. Find IRR of each, reject IRR < MARR
  3. Order remaining by rising investment
  4. Compare first two: (higher cost − lower cost)
  5. Incremental IRR ≥ MARR → costlier wins. Repeat to the end

Future worth

FW = PW(1+i)n   FW > 0 accept, < 0 reject, = 0 indifferent

10,000 @10% for 3 yrs = 13,310 (gain 3,310)

Benefit-cost ratio

B/C = PW benefits / PW costs   justified if B/C ≥ 1

Bridge: cost PW = 40,00,000 + 1,50,000(P/A,12%,15) = 50,21,635. Fuel savings PW = 57,82,556. B/C = 1.15, build it.

Drawbacks of BCA (public goods)

  • Benefits hard to measure in money
  • No market price for non-market benefits (clean air)
  • Valuation is subjective
  • Ignores who gains and who pays
  • Externalities hard to value
  • Future costs and benefits uncertain
  • Result depends on discount rate
  • Some values can't be put in rupees (heritage, dignity)

Sensitivity and break-even

  • Sensitivity: change one input (price, volume, cost, rate, life, salvage) and see the effect on NPW
  • Break-even = zero profit, zero loss

F = 2,00,000, P = 100, V = 60: Q = 2,00,000 / 40 = 5,000 units

Module 3: Time Value of Money

Rs 200 @10% becomes Rs 220 in 1 year. Money today is worth more because:

  • liquidity and chance to earn interest
  • people prefer present use, future is uncertain
  • capital is productive
  • inflation cuts future buying power

Simple interest: on original principal only. Compound: on principal + earlier interest.

P present, F future, A equal end-of-period amount, G gradient, i rate, n periods.

The 6 factors

F = P(1+i)n   (F/P)
P = F / (1+i)n   (P/F)
F = A[(1+i)n − 1] / i   (F/A)
A = F i / [(1+i)n − 1]   (A/F) sinking fund
P = A[(1+i)n − 1] / [i(1+i)n]   (P/A)
A = P i(1+i)n / [(1+i)n − 1]   (A/P) capital recovery

Examples

20,000 @18% 10 yrs: F = 1,04,677

1,00,000 in 10 yrs @15%: P = 24,718

10,000/yr, 25 yrs @20%: F = 47.2 lakh

5,00,000 in 15 yrs @18%: A = 8,201/yr

10,00,000/yr, 20 yrs @15%: P = 62.6 lakh

Loan 10,00,000 @18%, 15 yrs: A = 1,96,403/yr

Gradient series

Payments change by a fixed amount (200, 250, 300, 350).

A = A1 + G[1/i − n / ((1+i)n − 1)]

Annuity and annuity due

  • Annuity: equal payments, equal gaps, first payment at END of period 1
  • Annuity due: payments at the START. Take 1st payment alone, then ordinary annuity for the rest with N = payments − 1
PWdue = A + A(P/A, i, N−1)

Debt repayment

Principal + interest + schedule. On time = good credit, lower interest, no penalty.

Nominal vs effective rate

  • Nominal: stated yearly rate, ignores compounding
  • Effective: real yearly rate after compounding
ieff = (1 + r/m)m − 1

12% monthly: (1.01)12 − 1 = 12.68%

Module 4: Inflation, PW and Uncertainty

Inflation

Fall in buying power of money (deflation is the opposite).

  • Demand-pull: demand exceeds supply
  • Cost-push: input costs passed to buyers (oil)
  • Monetary: money grows faster than output

Effects: buying power falls, costs rise, interest rates rise, fixed incomes lose real value, comparisons get distorted.

Price indexes

Index = (price now / base price) × 100
f = (I2 / I1)1/n − 1
  • Price index: one item or group
  • Quantity index: physical volume, price fixed
  • CPI: fixed household basket, common inflation measure
  • WPI / PPI: wholesale or producer prices, often lead CPI
  • Commodity index: one item. Composite: weighted mix of many

Cement 300 → 390 gives index 130. Index 100 → 150 in 8 yrs gives 5.2%/yr.

Actual and constant money

Actual = Constant × (1+f)n
i = i′ + f + i′f

Golden rule: actual $ → nominal rate i. Constant $ → real rate i′.

1,00,000 @6% for 5 yrs needs 1,33,823. Real 8% + inflation 5% gives nominal 13.4%.

Different rates (energy 9%, labour 5%, revenue 4%): escalate each at its own rate, then add.

Present worth analysis

  • All flows to time 0. End-of-year convention
  • Fix the viewpoint (person, firm, society). Don't mix
  • Borrowed money: total-investment view leaves loan flows out. Investor view puts only own equity in and loan flows as outflows. Don't mix, double counting
  • Taxes: use after-tax. Depreciation cuts tax, raises cash flow
  • Criteria: fixed input max PW benefit, fixed output min PW cost, neither max net PW (if none > 0, do nothing)
  • Unequal lives: LCM of lives, or fixed study period with salvage
  • Perpetual: capitalized cost = P + A/i

Machine 5,00,000, returns 1,50,000 x 5 yrs @10%: PW = −5,00,000 + 1,50,000(3.791) = +68,650, OK.
Drainage: 20,00,000 + 1,00,000/0.08 = 32,50,000.

Uncertainty

  • Range: optimistic, most likely, pessimistic estimates
  • Probability: 0 to 1, all outcomes add to 1
  • Joint (independent): multiply, 0.40 × 0.50 = 0.20
  • Expected value = Σ p × outcome
  • Decision tree: roll back, EV at chance nodes, best branch at decisions
  • Risk: chance result differs from expected, esp. a loss
  • Risk vs return: more risk needs more return (raise MARR)
  • Simulation (Monte Carlo): random inputs, repeat many times, get a distribution
  • Real options: defer, expand or contract, abandon, switch

EV: 0.3(2,00,000) + 0.5(3,00,000) + 0.2(1,00,000) = 2,30,000

Module 5: Depreciation and Replacement

Depreciation

Fall in asset value from use, age, wear, obsolescence. Spreads cost over life. Non-cash. Book value is not market value.

B first cost, SV salvage, N life, BV book value.

  • Why: match cost to revenue, cut taxable income, fund replacement
  • Physical deterioration, or obsolescence (functional or economic)
  • Capital cost is spread. Operating cost is charged in full
  • Land and inventory are never depreciable. Buildings, machines, intangibles are
Tax shield = d × t   ATCF = (R − E − d)(1 − t) + d

d = 50,000 @30% tax saves 15,000.

Straight-line

d = (B − SV) / N   BVt = B − t d

1,00,000, SV 10,000, N 5: d = 18,000. BV: 82k, 64k, 46k, 28k, 10k

Declining balance

dt = R × BVt−1   BV = B(1−R)t
DDB: R = 2/N   150% DB: R = 1.5/N

DDB R = 0.4: 40,000 / 24,000 / 14,400 / 8,640 / yr 5 only 2,960 (stop at SV 10,000)

SL: equal each year, uses SV. DB: bigger early, SV is only a floor.

Tax rules

  • Based on full cost incl. installation. Assets in classes
  • Law fixes the method. Salvage often ignored. Land no
  • Timing conventions (half-year, half rate if used < 180 days)
  • Sale above BV = taxable gain (recapture)
  • Tax and book depreciation differ. India uses WDV on blocks

Replacement analysis

  • Defender = old asset. Challenger = best new one
  • Sunk cost ignored. Outsider view: defender's market value is its first cost
  • Replace for deterioration, obsolescence, inadequacy, savings
  1. Define problem
  2. Collect data
  3. Challenger's minimum cost life and EUAC
  4. Defender's marginal cost for next year
  5. MC > challenger EUAC: replace. Otherwise keep 1 more year
  6. Repeat every year
EUAC(N) = (P−SV)(A/P,i,N) + SV i + [ΣO&M(P/F)](A/P,i,N)
MC = (MVt−1 − MVt) + i MVt−1 + O&M

EUAC is U-shaped. Capital cost falls with age, O&M rises. Min cost life is where MC crosses EUAC.

P = 20,000, i = 10%. MC by year: 11,000 / 9,400 / 9,500 / 10,700 / 12,450

EUAC by life: 11,000 / 10,238 / 10,015 / 10,163 / 10,537

Min cost life = 3 years, EUAC 10,015

Defender MC = (8,000 − 6,000) + 800 + 6,500 = 9,300 < 10,015. Keep one more year.

Module 6: Accounting Basics

Record, classify, summarise, interpret, communicate. The language of business.

Balance sheet

Snapshot on one date.

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity
  • Assets: what it owns. Fixed (land, plant) and current (cash, stock, receivables)
  • Liabilities: what it owes. Long-term (> 1 yr) and current (< 1 yr)
  • Equity: owner's capital + retained profit

Income statement

Performance over a period: profit or loss?

Sales − direct costs = gross profit
Gross profit − operating, interest, tax = net profit

Balance sheet = photograph. Income statement = video.

Ratios

  • Liquidity: pay short-term bills? Current ratio = CA / CL
  • Profitability: gross profit, net profit, return on investment
  • Solvency: long-term survival. Debt-equity ratio
  • Activity: resource use. Stock turnover

Capital vs revenue

  • Capital: benefit > 1 year, creates an asset (CNC machine, shed, land, long-term loan). On balance sheet
  • Revenue: day-to-day (sales, material, wages, electricity, repairs). In income statement

Cost accounting

  • Finds the cost of each product, helps set price, controls waste, supports make-or-buy
  • Elements of cost: materials, labour, expenses

Direct vs indirect

Direct
traceable to one product, easy to measure, varies with output (material, labour, special tool)
Indirect
shared overhead, needs fair split, often steady (rent, electricity, supervisor, depreciation)
Total cost = direct + fair share of indirect

Allocation

  1. Collect indirect costs
  2. Pick a fair basis
  3. Share them out
  4. Add each share to direct cost
  • Rent → floor area
  • Electricity → machine hours
  • Supervisor → number of workers
  • Maintenance → machine hours used
  • Store-keeping → value or quantity of material

Methods: direct allocation, apportionment, activity-based costing (most accurate).
