    The Nature of Signals

    A signal is a function of time that carries information — much as an owl carries a letter through the night, so does a signal carry its message through the dimension of time.

    In the wizarding world of engineering, time is our parchment and the signal amplitude our ink. Every waveform tells a story.

    • Continuous-Time Signal \( x(t) \) — The variable \(t\) flows smoothly like a river, taking any real value.
    • Discrete-Time Signal \( x(n) \) — Here the variable \(n\) may only take integer values, as if hopping from stone to stone across a stream.

    Sorceries of Time: Shifting, Scaling & Reversal
    I. The Art of Time Shifting

    To shift a signal in time is to move its entire form — left or right along the temporal axis — as one might slide a painting along a gallery wall without altering the painting itself.

    Right Shift (Delay): \( y(t) = x(t - a) \) shifts \( x(t) \) to the right by \(a\) units.

    Left Shift (Advance): \( y(t) = x(t + a) \) shifts \( x(t) \) to the left by \(a\) units.
    — Original x(t) — Shifted y(t)
    II. The Compression and Expansion Spell

    Time scaling transforms the speed at which a signal unfolds. Replacing \(t\) with \(at\) compresses the signal; replacing with \(t/a\) expands it.

    \[ y(t) = x(at) \quad \begin{cases} |a| > 1 & \text{compresses by factor } a \\ |a| < 1 & \text{expands by factor } 1/a \end{cases} \]
    III. The Mirror Reversal

    Time reversal, \(y(t) = x(-t)\), is akin to holding a signal up to an enchanted looking glass — every point \(y(\tau) = x(-\tau)\) is reflected across the vertical axis. Key mappings: \(y(0)=x(0)\), \(y(1)=x(-1)\), \(y(2)=x(-2)\).

    \[ y(t) = x(-t) \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad \text{flip across the } t=0 \text{ axis} \]

    I. The Unit Step — u(t)

    The Unit Step is the most steadfast of signals — zero in the dark ages before \(t=0\), then rising to unity and holding forever after, like a torch lit at the stroke of midnight.

    \[ u(t) = \begin{cases} 1, & t \ge 0 \\ 0, & t < 0 \end{cases} \]

    The Impulse is the most powerful and mysterious of signals — an infinite spike at a single instant, with total area precisely equal to unity. It is the Avada Kedavra of signal theory: singular, total, and exact.

    \[ \delta(t) = \begin{cases} \infty, & t=0 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(t)\,dt = 1 \]
    III. The Rectangular Pulse — Rect(t/τ)

    Denoted \(\text{Rect}(t/\tau)\), this signal stands proud at unit height over the interval \(-\tau/2 \le t \le \tau/2\), giving a total width of \(\tau\).

    • \( 3\,\Delta(t/8) \) — peak 3 at origin, base from \(-8\) to \(8\)
    • \( 5\,\Delta(t/3) \) — peak 5 at origin, base from \(-3\) to \(3\)

    Chapter The Fourth
    The Fourier Grimoire & LTI Systems
    I. LTI Systems — The Order of the Impulse Response

    A Linear Time-Invariant system is fully characterised by its impulse response \(h(t)\). If you feed it an impulse \(\delta(t)\), it reveals its true nature \(h(t)\). For any other input, the output is the mystical operation of convolution:

    \[ y(t) = x(t) * h(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)\, h(t-\tau)\, d\tau \]
    II. The Fourier Transform — Revealing Hidden Frequencies

    The Fourier Transform is perhaps the greatest incantation in all of signal sorcery — it transmutes a time-domain signal into its frequency-domain spirit, revealing which harmonic frequencies compose it.

    \[ X(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\, e^{-j\omega t}\, dt \]
    III. The Sacred Properties
    • Time Shifting: \( x(t-\tau) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}} X(\omega)\,e^{-j\omega\tau} \) — a shift in time becomes a phase rotation in frequency
    • Frequency Shifting: \( x(t)\,e^{j\omega_0 t} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}} X(\omega - \omega_0) \) — multiply by a complex exponential, shift in frequency
    • Convolution: \( x(t)*h(t) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}} X(\omega)\cdot H(\omega) \) — convolution becomes multiplication!
    • Multiplication: \( x(t)\cdot h(t) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}} \tfrac{1}{2\pi}[X(\omega)*H(\omega)] \) — multiplication becomes convolution
    \[ \text{Sinc}(x) = \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}, \quad e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2} \]
    IV. The Three Great Filters
    • Low Pass Filter (LPF): Permits low-frequency components \(|\omega| \le \omega_c\) to pass; blocks the high-frequency noise like a soundproof dungeon door.
    • High Pass Filter (HPF): Blocks low frequencies; admits only the high-frequency spirits above \(\omega_c\).
    • Band Pass Filter (BPF): A narrow window of acceptance — only frequencies within a specific band are permitted entry.

    The Modulation Arts — DSBSC

    Modulation is the alchemical process of encoding the humble message signal \(m(t)\) upon a high-frequency carrier \(\cos(\omega_c t)\), that it may travel vast distances — much as an owl bears a letter across the continents.

    I. DSBSC — Double Sideband Suppressed Carrier
    \[ s(t) = m(t) \cdot \cos(\omega_c t) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}} \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[M(\omega-\omega_c) + M(\omega+\omega_c)\bigr] \]

    The original baseband signal of bandwidth \(B\) is replicated about \(\pm\omega_c\), producing a bandpass signal of bandwidth \(\mathbf{2B}\). The carrier frequency itself is suppressed — hence the name.

    II. Power in the Realms
    • Power of carrier \(\cos(\omega_c t) = \frac{1}{2}\)
    • Power of DSBSC signal \(= \frac{P_m}{2}\)
    • Power of upper sideband \(= \frac{P_m}{4}\); likewise lower sideband \(= \frac{P_m}{4}\)
    • Power of \(A\cdot m(t)\cos(\omega_c t) = A^2\dfrac{P_m}{2}\)
    III. Demodulation — Recovering the Message

    To recover \(m(t)\) from the modulated signal, multiply again by the carrier and pass through a Low Pass Filter:

    \[ m(t)\cos(\omega_c t) \times \cos(\omega_c t) = \frac{m(t)}{2} + \underbrace{\frac{m(t)\cos(2\omega_c t)}{2}}_{\text{blocked by LPF}} \xrightarrow{\text{LPF}} \frac{m(t)}{2} \]
    IV. The Phase Error Curse

    If the local oscillator suffers a phase error \(\theta\), using \(\cos(\omega_c t + \theta)\) instead of \(\cos(\omega_c t)\):

    \[ \text{After LPF}: \quad y(t) = \frac{m(t)\cos\theta}{2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad P_{\text{out}} = \frac{P_m \cos^2\theta}{4} \]

    A constant phase merely scales the output — demodulation succeeds. But a frequency error causes \(m(t)\cos(40t)\), which destroys the message shape entirely.

    Chapter The Seventh
    Power, Parseval & The Fourier Series
    I. Signal Power

    The power of a periodic signal \(x(t)\) with period \(T_0\) is the mean-squared value over one full cycle:

    \[ P = \frac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0} x^2(t)\,dt \]

    For sinusoidal signals, this simplifies beautifully: the power of \(A\cos(\omega_0 t)\) is simply \(\frac{A^2}{2}\).

    Power of \(3\cos(40t) + 4\sin(20t) + 3\cos(70t)\) \(= \dfrac{9}{2} + \dfrac{16}{2} + \dfrac{9}{2} = \mathbf{17}\)
    II. Parseval's Theorem — The Great Bridge

    Parseval's Theorem bridges the time and frequency domains — the total energy is preserved under the Fourier Transform:

    \[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x(t)|^2\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|X(\omega)|^2\,d\omega \]

    Here \(|X(\omega)|^2\) is the Energy Spectral Density — the distribution of energy across frequencies.

    III. The Fourier Series — Decomposing Periodic Spells

    Any periodic signal may be expressed as an infinite sum of harmonics. In the Exponential form:

    \[ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k\,e^{jk\omega_0 t}, \quad C_k = \frac{1}{T_0}\int_{-T_0/2}^{T_0/2} x(t)\,e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt \]

    In trigonometric form, the coefficients relate as: \(a_0 = C_0\), \(a_k = C_k + C_{-k}\), \(b_k = j(C_k - C_{-k})\).

    IV. DSBSC Modulator — The Square-Law Device

    A non-linear device with law \(y = x^2\), fed input \(x(t)=m(t)+\cos(\omega_c t)\), produces:

    \[ y(t) = m^2(t) + \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{\cos(2\omega_c t)}{2} + 2m(t)\cos(\omega_c t) \]

    A BPF centred at \(\omega_c\) extracts the DSBSC term \(2m(t)\cos(\omega_c t)\), discarding the rest. Note that a cubic-law device \((y=x^3)\) cannot cleanly generate DSBSC due to intermodulation products.

    • Sampling Function: Defined as \( Sa(x)=\frac{\sin(x)}{x} \).
      Example: \( Sa(5t)=\frac{\sin(5t)}{5t} \). Zero crossings occur when \( 5t=n\pi \), thus \( t=\frac{n\pi}{5} \).

    Advanced Modulators & Frequency Errors
    I. The Curse of Frequency Error

    While a static phase error merely dims the magic, a frequency error shatters it entirely. If the local oscillator strays by a frequency of \(40\) rad/sec, yielding \(2\cos((\omega_c + 40)t)\):

    \[ m(t)\cos(\omega_c t) \times 2\cos((\omega_c + 40)t) = m(t)[\cos((2\omega_c + 40)t) + \cos(40t)] \]

    After the Low Pass Filter blocks the high-frequency components, we are left with \(m(t)\cos(40t)\). Because the error \(40t\) varies with time, the envelope shape of the message is destroyed. Demodulation is rendered impossible.

    II. Diode Logic (The Gates of Current)

    Before weaving complex hardware, one must recall the basic laws of diodes from the ancient texts of Class 12th:

    • Diode ON (Short Circuit): P-side is at a higher potential than the N-side.
    • Diode OFF (Open Circuit): P-side is at a lower potential than the N-side.
    III. The Switching Modulator

    A bridge circuit acting as a gatekeeper, driven by a massive carrier signal \(A_c\cos(\omega_c t)\).

    • Positive half-cycle (\(\cos > 0\)): All diodes are ON. The output passes through: \(y(t) = m(t)\).
    • Negative half-cycle (\(\cos < 0\)): All diodes are OFF. The output goes dark: \(y(t) = 0\).

    This is modeled by multiplying \(m(t)\) by a switching pulse train \(w(t)\), where \(w(t) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{\pi}\cos(\omega_c t) - \dots\). Passing the product \(y(t) = m(t)w(t)\) through a Band Pass Filter (BPF) centered at \(\omega_c\) successfully yields the DSBSC signal: \(\frac{2}{\pi}m(t)\cos(\omega_c t)\).

    IV. The Ring Modulator (Double Balanced)

    A continuous ring of four diodes. Instead of merely turning off during the negative half-cycle, the crossed connections elegantly reverse the polarity!

    • Positive half-cycle: Diodes \(D_1\) and \(D_2\) are ON. Output \(y(t) = m(t)\).
    • Negative half-cycle: Diodes \(D_3\) and \(D_4\) are ON. Polarity flips: \(y(t) = -m(t)\).

    Mathematically, \(y(t) = m(t) \cdot Z(t)\), where \(Z(t)\) is a square wave alternating strictly between \(+1\) and \(-1\). Because there is no DC offset (\(1/2\)) in the Fourier series of \(Z(t)\), a BPF at \(\omega_c\) cleanly isolates the pure DSBSC signal: \(\frac{4}{\pi}m(t)\cos(\omega_c t)\).

    Chapter The Tenth
    The Envelope Detector & DSBFC
    I. The Linearity of AM

    DSBSC is a Linear Modulation Scheme. It perfectly satisfies the ancient law of superposition: if two separate messages are combined \(m_1(t) + m_2(t)\), the modulated result is simply the sum of their individual modulations.

    II. The Flaw of the Envelope Detector

    At the receiver, generating a perfectly synchronized carrier to demodulate DSBSC is a costly and complex magical working. An Envelope Detector offers a cheap, unsynchronized alternative by simply tracing the outer peaks (the envelope) of the wave.

    The fatal flaw: The envelope of a DSBSC signal is \(|m(t)|\). If your message dips below zero, the detector violently flips the negative peaks to positive, destroying the true shape of the message!
    III. The Solution: DSBFC (Full Carrier)

    To prevent the signal from ever dropping below zero, we inject a massive DC constant (\(A\)) into the message. The carrier is no longer suppressed; it is fully present.

    \[ x(t) = [A + m(t)]\cos(\omega_c t) \]

    The Strict Rule: For the envelope detector to work flawlessly, the added constant must be greater than or equal to the absolute maximum negative dip of the message: \( A \ge |m(t)|_{max} \).

    IV. The Three Forms of DSBFC
    • Direct Form: \( x(t) = [A + m(t)]\cos(\omega_c t) \)
    • Modulation Index (\(\mu\)): Defined as \(\mu = \frac{|m(t)|_{max}}{A}\).
      The signal becomes \( x(t) = A[1 + \mu\hat{m}(t)]\cos(\omega_c t) \), where \(\hat{m}(t)\) is the normalized message with a peak of 1.
    • Amplitude Sensitivity (\(K_a\)): Defined as \(K_a = \frac{1}{A}\).
      The signal becomes \( x(t) = A[1 + K_a m(t)]\cos(\omega_c t) \).
    V. Power & Efficiency

    The pure carrier term \(A\cos(\omega_c t)\) contains no actual information—it is "non-intelligent" power. The information lives purely in the sidebands.

    \[ \eta = \frac{\text{Sideband Power}}{\text{Total Power}} = \frac{\mu^2 P_{\hat{m}}}{1 + \mu^2 P_{\hat{m}}} \times 100 \]

    When a carrier is modulated by several frequencies at once (e.g., \( A_{m1}\cos(\omega_{m1}t) + A_{m2}\cos(\omega_{m2}t) \)), the individual indices merge into a Total Modulation Index.

    \( \mu_T = \sqrt{\mu_1^2 + \mu_2^2 + \mu_3^2 \dots} \)

    \( P_T = P_c(1 + \frac{\mu_T^2}{2}) \)

    Power Saved: If switching from DSBFC to DSBSC, the power saved is simply the carrier power: \( \% \text{Saved} = \frac{P_c}{P_T} \times 100 \).

    IV. Advanced SSB Generation
    • Frequency Discrimination (Filter): Pass DSB-SC through a BPF to slice off one sideband. It requires a sharp "brick-wall" filter and only works well if the signal has a frequency gap around DC (like human speech).
    • Phase Discrimination (Hilbert Transform): The Hilbert Transform \( \hat{m}(t) \) shifts positive frequencies by \( -90^\circ \) (\( -j \)) and negative frequencies by \( +90^\circ \) (\( +j \)).

    Chapter The Seventeenth
    Advanced Sideband Sorcery
    I. The Peril of SSB Phase Error

    To demodulate an SSB signal \( m(t)\cos(\omega_c t) \pm \hat{m}(t)\sin(\omega_c t) \), we use synchronous demodulation. However, if the receiver's local oscillator suffers a constant phase error (\( \theta \)), the Low Pass Filter output becomes:

    \( y(t) = \frac{1}{2}m(t)\cos(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\hat{m}(t)\sin(-\theta) \)

    Unlike DSB-SC (where phase error merely weakens the magic), an error in SSB introduces severe distortion by leaking the Hilbert transformed signal \( \hat{m}(t) \) into the output. A perfect Phase-Locked Loop is required to prevent this disaster.

    II. Single Sideband With Carrier (SSBWC)

    By injecting a massive carrier \( A\cos(\omega_c t) \) into the SSB signal, it can be demodulated cheaply using an Asynchronous Envelope Detector. The envelope is mathematically \( \sqrt{(A + m(t))^2 + (\hat{m}(t))^2} \).

    Because \( A \gg |m(t)|_{max} \), the squared Hilbert term becomes negligible. Using the binomial approximation \( (1+x)^{1/2} \approx 1 + x/2 \), the envelope simplifies to \( E(t) \approx A + m(t) \). The trade-off for this cheap receiver is massive power wastage at the transmitter.

    III. Vestigial Sideband (VSB) & Television

    SSB is practically impossible to generate for signals that have high energy near DC (zero frequency), such as Video Signals. VSB is the practical compromise heavily used in Television communication. It transmits one full sideband and a small "vestige" (\( W_v \)) of the other.

    Vestigial Filters & Equalizers
    I. The VSB Filter Condition

    To craft a VSB signal, the message is modulated into DSB-SC and then passed through a "Vestige Filter" with a frequency response \( H(\omega) \). For the receiver to perfectly recover the message without distortion, the filter must exhibit strict symmetry around the carrier frequency:

    \( H(\omega - \omega_c) + H(\omega + \omega_c) = \text{Constant} \quad \text{for } |\omega| < W_m \)

    If the chosen VSB filter is flawed and fails the constant sum condition, the recovered message will be horribly distorted. To fix this, an Equalizer Filter is placed at the receiver after demodulation. Its frequency response must be the exact inverse of the error:

    \( H_e(\omega) = \frac{1}{H(\omega - \omega_c) + H(\omega + \omega_c)} \quad \text{for } |\omega| < W_m \)

    Chapter The Nineteenth
    Angle Modulation & Carson's Rule
    I. The Twin Spells: PM vs. FM

    In Angle Modulation, the amplitude remains perfectly constant. The message signal \( m(t) \) instead alters the phase or the frequency of the carrier.

    • Phase Modulation (PM): Phase changes linearly with the message.
      Equation: \( \phi(t) = 2\pi f_c t + k_p m(t) \)
      Max Phase Deviation: \( \Delta\phi_{max} = k_p |m(t)|_{max} \)
    • Frequency Modulation (FM): Frequency changes linearly with the message.
      Instantaneous Freq: \( f_i(t) = f_c + k_f m(t) \)
      Max Frequency Deviation: \( \Delta f_{max} = k_f |m(t)|_{max} \)
      Phase Equation: \( \phi(t) = 2\pi f_c t + 2\pi k_f \int m(\tau) d\tau \)
    II. Power & Carson's Rule

    Unlike AM, the total power of an angle-modulated signal is always constant, regardless of the message: \( P_T = \frac{A_c^2}{2} \). While the bandwidth is technically infinite, 98% of the power is contained within Carson's Rule:

    \( BW \approx 2(\beta + 1)f_m = 2(\Delta f + f_m) \)
    III. Narrowband vs. Wideband
    • Narrowband (NBFM/NBPM): Occurs when \( \beta \ll 1 \). Bandwidth is tiny (\( \approx 2f_m \)). It behaves much like DSBFC AM, but the sidebands are shifted by \( 90^\circ \).
    • Wideband (WBFM/WBPM): Occurs when \( \beta > 1 \). You must invoke Carson's Rule.
    IV. Multipliers vs. Mixers

    To increase frequency, you may use a Multiplier or a Mixer. A Frequency Multiplier (\( \times n \)) multiplies the carrier (\( n f_c \)), frequency deviation (\( n \Delta f \)), and modulation index (\( n \beta \)), but leaves the message bandwidth (\( f_m \)) untouched. A Frequency Mixer (Heterodyning) shifts ONLY the center carrier frequency (\( f_c + f_i \)); all deviations and indices remain completely untouched.

    Superheterodyne & Demodulation
    I. The Superheterodyne Receiver

    A Frequency Converter (Mixer) translates a high carrier frequency (\( f_c \)) down to a lower, manageable Intermediate Frequency (\( f_{IF} \)) using a Local Oscillator (\( f_{LO} \)).

    • Down Conversion: \( f_{IF} = |f_{LO} - f_c| \)

      Formula (High-side tuning): \( f_{image} = f_c + 2f_{IF} \)

    To defeat the image frequency, a high-quality RF Band Pass Filter is placed at the antenna. Its quality factor is \( Q = \frac{\text{Center Frequency}}{\text{Bandwidth}} \).

    Two primary rituals exist to extract the message \( m(t) \) from an FM or PM signal:

    • Differentiator + Envelope Detector: Differentiating an FM signal converts its frequency variations into amplitude variations. An Envelope Detector traces the amplitude, and a DC block removes the constant \( \omega_c \), leaving \( 2\pi k_f m(t) \). (For PM, this yields the derivative \( k_p m'(t) \), which must then be integrated).
    • Phase Locked Loop (PLL): A closed-loop system (Comparator, LPF, and VCO) that continuously tracks the phase of the incoming signal, directly outputting \( m(t) \) without differentiation.
    III. Defeating Noise

    Because FM/PM stores information in frequency/phase, amplitude noise distorts the spell. A Band Pass Limiter clips the noisy signal into a square wave and filters it back into a clean sine wave, stripping away the amplitude noise. Pre-emphasis & De-emphasis are used to boost high frequencies before transmission and attenuate them upon reception to neutralize channel noise.

    I. The Infinite Sidebands

    When a single tone \( A_m \cos(\omega_m t) \) is frequency modulated, the resulting wave shatters into an infinite spectrum governed by Bessel Functions of the first kind (\( J_n \)).

    II. The Laws of Bessel Power
    • Conservation of Power: Total power remains constant (\( P_T = \frac{A_c^2}{2} \)). Mathematically, the sum of the squares of all coefficients equals one: \( \sum [J_n(\beta)]^2 = 1 \).
    • Symmetry Property: \( J_{-n}(\beta) = (-1)^n J_n(\beta) \). Even sidebands are perfectly symmetrical; odd sidebands are inverted.
    • Carrier Suppression: At specific modulation indices known as "eigenvalues" (\( \beta = 2.44, 5.52, 8.65 \dots \)), the carrier component vanishes entirely because \( J_0(\beta) = 0 \).

    Receiver Architecture & Phase Locks
    I. Up vs. Down Conversion

    A Superheterodyne Receiver uses a mixer to translate a carrier frequency (\( f_c \)) to an Intermediate Frequency (\( f_{IF} \)).

    • Up Conversion: \( f_{LO} > f_c \). Generally preferred because the required tuning capacitor ratio is more feasible to manufacture.
    • Down Conversion: \( f_{LO} < f_c \).
    Up Conversion: \( f_{image} = f_c + 2f_{IF} \)

    Down Conversion: \( f_{image} = f_c - 2f_{IF} \)
    II. Image Rejection Ratio (IRR)

    To mitigate the image frequency curse, an RF Band Pass Filter is placed before the mixer. The effectiveness of this filter is determined by its Quality Factor (\( Q \)) and the normalized gap (\( \alpha \)) between the carrier and image frequencies.

    \( \alpha = \frac{f_{img}}{f_c} - \frac{f_c}{f_{img}} \)

    \( IRR = \sqrt{1 + \alpha^2 Q^2} \)

    A larger gap between \( f_{img} \) and \( f_c \) results in a higher \( \alpha \), which yields a vastly superior Image Rejection Ratio.

    III. Phase Locked Loops (PLL)

    A PLL is a negative feedback system where the phase and frequency of the feedback signal seek to perfectly match the incoming signal. It consists of a Multiplier (phase comparator), a Loop Filter (Low Pass Filter), and a Voltage Controlled Oscillator (VCO). The Capture Range is the frequency band where the PLL can acquire a lock, while the Lock Range is the band where it can maintain it.

    The Laws of Probability
    I. The Fundamentals of Chance
    • Random Experiment (RE): An event with a random outcome drawn from a known, fixed set (e.g., rolling a die).
    • Sample Space: The complete set of all possible outcomes.
    • Event: A specific subset of the sample space (e.g., "Rolling an even number").
    II. Algebra of Events
    • Intersection (\( A \cap B \)): The probability that Event A and Event B occur together.
    • Union (\( A \cup B \)): The probability of Event A or Event B.
    • Complements (\( \overline{A} \)): The probability of Event A not occurring.
    • Neither A nor B: Represented as \( \overline{A \cup B} \) or \( \overline{A} \cap \overline{B} \).
    III. Types of Events

    Independent Events do not affect one another: \( P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B) \).
    Mutually Exclusive Events can never occur together: \( P(A \cap B) = 0 \).
    Exhaustive Events collectively cover the entire Sample Space.

    IV. Conditional Probability & Bayes' Theorem

    Conditional Probability defines the chance of Event A occurring given that Event B has already transpired.

    \( P(A/B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \)

    \( P(A \cap B) = P(A/B)P(B) = P(B/A)P(A) \)

    If \( P(A) = 1/3 \), \( P(B) = 1/4 \), and \( P(A/B) = 1/6 \), what is \( P(B/A) \)?

    Appendix V
    Pulse Transformations (PAM & PWM)
    Pulse Amplitude Modulation (PAM)

    Pulse-amplitude modulation is a form of signal modulation where the message information is encoded in the amplitude of a series of signal pulses. It is an analog pulse modulation scheme in which the amplitude of train of carrier pulse is varied according to the sample value of the message signal.

    The signal is sampled at regular intervals and each sample is made proportional to the magnitude of the signal at the instant of sampling. These sampled pulses may then be sent either directly by a channel to the receiving end or may be made to modulate using a carrier wave before transmission.

    For the generation of a PAM signal we use a flat top type PAM scheme because during the transmission, the noise is interfered at top of the transmission pulse which can be easily removed if the PAM pulse in flat top. Waveforms showing pulse carriers whose amplitude is modulated by message. A simple way to implement this is to allow the message to be fed as the input to a switch and the switch ON/OFF time is controlled by the pulses at sampling frequency. The demodulation of PAM waveform can be implemented by using a lowpass filter which passes message signal frequencies but blocks the carrier signal.

    Pulse Width Modulation (PWM)

    The PWM is also known as pulse duration modulation. It modulates the time parameter of the pulses. The width of PWM pulses varies. The amplitude is constant; width of the pulse is proportional to the amplitude of the modulating signal.

    Bandwidth on transmission channel depends on rise time of the pulse. The demodulation circuit used is a simple filter circuit that demodulator the PWM signal and gives the original message input.
