1. Transmission Lines Overview

A transmission line consists of two or more parallel conductors used to transmit electromagnetic energy and signals between a source (generator, transmitter) and a load (antenna, receiver, circuit). Typical physical configurations include:

  • Coaxial Cable: Inner conductor surrounded by a concentric dielectric sheath and metallic outer braid shield. Widely used for broadband RF.
  • Two-Wire Line (Twin-Lead): Two parallel insulated cylindrical conductors.
  • Planar / Microstrip Line: Thin metallic strip separated from a conducting ground plane by a dielectric substrate. Standard for high-frequency microwave integrated circuits (MMICs).
Distributed vs. Lumped Circuits: When signal frequency is high enough that the physical line length $l \ge \lambda/20$, voltage and current vary along the spatial dimension $z$, requiring distributed parameter analysis ($R, L, G, C$ per unit length).

2. Distributed Parameters & Telegrapher’s Equations

A differential segment of length $\Delta z$ is modeled with four distributed parameters:

  • $R$ = Series resistance per unit length ($\Omega/\text{m}$) due to finite conductor conductivity $\sigma_c$.
  • $L$ = Series loop inductance per unit length ($\text{H/m}$) due to magnetic flux linkage.
  • $G$ = Shunt conductance per unit length ($\text{S/m}$) due to dielectric leakage current.
  • $C$ = Shunt capacitance per unit length ($\text{F/m}$) between the two conductors.
Time-Harmonic Telegrapher’s Equations
$$-\frac{dV(z)}{dz} = (R + j\omega L)I(z)$$ $$-\frac{dI(z)}{dz} = (G + j\omega C)V(z)$$
Wave Equations for Voltage & Current

Differentiating again gives second-order wave equations:

$$\frac{d^2 V(z)}{dz^2} - \gamma^2 V(z) = 0, \quad \frac{d^2 I(z)}{dz^2} - \gamma^2 I(z) = 0$$
Complex Propagation Constant ($\gamma$)
$$\gamma = \alpha + j\beta = \sqrt{(R + j\omega L)(G + j\omega C)}$$

Where $\alpha$ is the attenuation constant ($\text{Np/m}$ or $\text{dB/m}$) and $\beta$ is the phase constant ($\text{rad/m}$).

Characteristic Impedance ($Z_0$)

Ratio of forward-traveling voltage wave to forward-traveling current wave:

$$Z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{R + j\omega L}{G + j\omega C}} \quad [\Omega]$$

4. Reflection Coefficient & Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR)

Voltage Reflection Coefficient at Load ($\Gamma_L$)

The ratio of reflected voltage wave amplitude to incident voltage wave amplitude at the load terminal $z = 0$:

$$\Gamma_L = \frac{V_0^-}{V_0^+} = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} = |\Gamma_L|e^{j\theta_\Gamma}$$
Special Boundary Terminations
  • Matched Load ($Z_L = Z_0$): $\Gamma_L = 0$. No reflections, all power delivered to load (flat line).
  • Short Circuit ($Z_L = 0$): $\Gamma_L = -1 = 1 e^{j\pi}$. Complete reflection, voltage node at load.
  • Open Circuit ($Z_L = \infty$): $\Gamma_L = +1 = 1 e^{j0}$. Complete reflection, voltage antinode at load.
  • Purely Reactive Load ($Z_L = \pm jX$): $|\Gamma_L| = 1$. Total reflection of power.
Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR or $s$)

Measures the ratio of maximum voltage amplitude to minimum voltage amplitude along the line:

$$s = \text{VSWR} = \frac{V_{\max}}{V_{\min}} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma_L|}{1 - |\Gamma_L|} \quad (1 \le s < \infty)$$

Inverting for reflection coefficient magnitude:

$$|\Gamma_L| = \frac{s - 1}{s + 1}$$

5. Input Impedance & Impedance Matching

General Input Impedance Equation

At a distance $l$ from a load $Z_L$ on a lossless line with characteristic impedance $Z_0$:

$$Z_{\text{in}}(l) = Z_0 \left[ \frac{Z_L + j Z_0 \tan(\beta l)}{Z_0 + j Z_L \tan(\beta l)} \right]$$
Extreme Impedance Transformations
$$Z_{\text{in,max}} = s Z_0, \quad Z_{\text{in,min}} = \frac{Z_0}{s}$$

6. Transit Time Effect in Transmission Lines

The transit time delay $t_{\text{transit}}$ experienced by an electromagnetic wave traveling across line length $l$ at phase velocity $v_p$:

$$t_{\text{transit}} = \frac{l}{v_p}$$

Transit time effects become significant when the line length is comparable to a substantial fraction of the wavelength:

$$l \ge \frac{\lambda}{20} \iff t_{\text{transit}} \ge \frac{T}{20}$$

When this threshold is crossed, ordinary lumped-circuit Kirchhoff's Voltage and Current Laws (KVL/KCL) fail, requiring distributed transmission line wave analysis.

7. Rectangular Waveguides & Modes

A rectangular waveguide is a hollow metallic pipe of rectangular cross-section (broad wall dimension $a$, narrow wall dimension $b$, with $a > b$) used to guide microwave and millimeter-wave signals via successive reflections from metallic walls.

Supported Modes
  • Transverse Electric (TE) Modes ($E_z = 0, H_z \ne 0$): The electric field is entirely transverse to the direction of propagation ($z$).
  • Transverse Magnetic (TM) Modes ($H_z = 0, E_z \ne 0$): The magnetic field is entirely transverse to the direction of propagation ($z$).
  • TEM Mode Is NOT Supported: A hollow single-conductor waveguide cannot support Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) waves ($E_z = 0, H_z = 0$). By Gauss's Law, static field lines in a closed metallic boundary without an isolated center conductor must be zero everywhere.
Boundary Conditions on Metallic Waveguide Walls
$$E_t = 0 \quad \text{[Tangential electric field vanishes on conductor walls]}$$ $$B_n = 0 \quad \text{[Normal magnetic flux vanishes on conductor walls]}$$

8. Cutoff Frequency, Cutoff Wavelength & Dominant Mode

Cutoff Frequency Formula ($f_{c,mn}$)

The operating frequency below which electromagnetic waves undergo rapid exponential attenuation (evanescent mode) and above which wave propagation occurs:

$$f_{c,mn} = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{\mu_r \varepsilon_r}} \sqrt{\left(\frac{m}{a}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{n}{b}\right)^2}$$

Where $m, n$ are integers representing the number of half-wave field variations along dimensions $a$ and $b$.

Cutoff Wavelength ($\lambda_{c,mn}$)
$$\lambda_{c,mn} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{m}{a}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{n}{b}\right)^2}}$$
Dominant Mode ($\text{TE}_{10}$)

The dominant mode is defined as the mode having the lowest cutoff frequency (or longest cutoff wavelength). For standard rectangular waveguide where $a > b$:

$$\text{Dominant Mode: } \mathbf{TE}_{10} \quad (m=1, n=0)$$ $$f_{c,10} = \frac{c}{2a}, \quad \lambda_{c,10} = 2a$$
💡 Broad Wall Requirement: For the dominant $\text{TE}_{10}$ mode to propagate at operating frequency $f$ ($\lambda_0 = c/f$):
$$f > f_c \iff a > \frac{\lambda_0}{2}$$
Degenerate Modes

Modes that share identical cutoff frequencies are called degenerate modes. In a rectangular guide, $\text{TE}_{11}$ and $\text{TM}_{11}$ are always degenerate.

Guided Wavelength ($\lambda_g$)

The wavelength of the guided wave measured along the longitudinal axis of propagation:

$$\lambda_g = \frac{\lambda_0}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{f_c}{f}\right)^2}} = \frac{\lambda_0}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_c}\right)^2}} > \lambda_0$$
  • As operating frequency approaches cutoff ($f \to f_c$): $\lambda_g \to \infty$.
  • At very high frequencies ($f \gg f_c$): $\lambda_g \approx \lambda_0$.

9. Phase Velocity ($v_p$) vs. Group Velocity ($v_g$)

Phase Velocity ($v_p$)

Velocity at which a point of constant phase advances through the waveguide:

$$v_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac{c}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{f_c}{f}\right)^2}} > c$$
Notice that $v_p$ is always strictly greater than the speed of light $c$! This does not violate Einstein's theory of relativity because phase velocity carries no energy or information.
Group Velocity ($v_g$)

Velocity at which electromagnetic energy and information travel:

$$v_g = \frac{d\omega}{d\beta} = c\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{f_c}{f}\right)^2} < c$$
Fundamental Velocity Product Relation
$$v_p \cdot v_g = c^2$$

10. Core Exam Derivations

Derivation 1: Transmission Line Equations from Distributed Parameters

Apply KVL and KCL to an elemental line length $\Delta z$:

$$\text{KVL: } v(z, t) - R\Delta z\,i(z, t) - L\Delta z\,\frac{\partial i(z, t)}{\partial t} - v(z+\Delta z, t) = 0$$ Dividing by $\Delta z \to 0$ in sinusoidal steady-state: $$\frac{dV(z)}{dz} = -(R + j\omega L)I(z)$$ $$\text{KCL: } i(z, t) - G\Delta z\,v(z+\Delta z, t) - C\Delta z\,\frac{\partial v(z+\Delta z, t)}{\partial t} - i(z+\Delta z, t) = 0$$ $$\frac{dI(z)}{dz} = -(G + j\omega C)V(z)$$
Derivation 2: VSWR in terms of Load Reflection Coefficient

Total voltage wave along line: $V(z') = V_0^+ e^{j\beta z'}(1 + \Gamma_L e^{-j2\beta z'})$.

$$V_{\max} = |V_0^+|(1 + |\Gamma_L|)$$ $$V_{\min} = |V_0^+|(1 - |\Gamma_L|)$$ $$\text{VSWR} = s = \frac{V_{\max}}{V_{\min}} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma_L|}{1 - |\Gamma_L|}$$
Derivation 3: Phase Velocity $v_p > c$ in a Waveguide

Dispersion relation in waveguide: $\beta = \sqrt{\omega^2\mu\varepsilon - k_c^2} = \frac{\omega}{c}\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2}$.

$$v_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac{\omega}{\frac{\omega}{c}\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2}} = \frac{c}{\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2}}$$ Since $\sqrt{1 - (f_c/f)^2} < 1$ for propagating modes ($f > f_c$), the denominator is less than 1, proving $v_p > c$.
