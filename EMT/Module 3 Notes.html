Transmission Lines and Waveguide

Short notes on Transmission Lines

Transmission lines are commonly used in power distribution (at low frequencies) and in 
communications (at high frequencies). Transmission lines such as twisted-pair and coaxial cables 
(thinnet and thicknet) are used in computer networks such as the Ethernet and the Internet. A 
transmission line basically consists of two or more parallel conductors used to connect a source to a 
load. The source may be a hydroelectric generator, a transmitter, or an oscillator; the load may be a 
factory, an antenna, or an oscilloscope. Typical transmission lines include coaxial cable, a two-wire 
line, a parallel-plate or planar line, a wire above the conducting plane, and a microstrip line.

Waveguide: A waveguide is a hollow metallic structure (usually rectangular or circular in cross￾section) that guides electromagnetic waves from one point to another by reflecting them within its 
walls.
A rectangular waveguide is a hollow metallic device with four sides closed and two sides open. It 
can be used as : 
1. a radiator
2. a high pass filter
3. a transmission line
4. a feed element to antennas
A hollow rectangular waveguide supports only TE (Transverse Electric Ez = 0) and TM
Transverse Magnetic Hz = 0) waves/modes and it does not support TEM mode.
A parallel plane waveguide consists of two infinite conducting planes separated by a dielectric 
medium. It supports the propagation of TE (Transverse Electric) and TM (Transverse Magnetic)
modes between the planes.
The cutoff frequency is the operating frequency below which attenuation occurs and above which 
propagation takes place.

guide, which differs from the free-space wavelength (λ₀) because the wave is “guided” and partially 
reflected within the walls.
