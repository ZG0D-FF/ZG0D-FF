Ampère's Circuit Law – Maxwell's Equation

Definition: Ampère's circuit law states that the line integral of H around a closed path equals the net current enclosed by the path.
$$\oint_L\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=I_{enc}$$
  • H – Magnetic field intensity
  • dl – Differential length element
  • Ienc – Enclosed current

Important Notes

Ampère's law is similar to Gauss's law because it is easily applied for symmetrical current distributions.

The equation always remains valid, but it is mainly used for finding H when symmetry exists.

It is also a special case of Biot–Savart's law.

Maxwell's Third Equation

Applying Stokes' theorem:

$$I_{enc}=\oint_L\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} =\int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Also,

$$I_{enc}=\int_S\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Comparing both gives

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}$$

This is the third Maxwell's equation in differential form.

Since

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}\neq0$$

the magnetostatic field is not conservative.

Applications

Used for symmetrical current distributions.

  • Infinite line current
  • Infinite sheet current
  • Infinite coaxial transmission line
$$\oint_L\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=I_{enc}$$

For symmetrical distributions, H is either parallel or perpendicular to dl. When H is parallel to dl, its magnitude remains constant.

Quick Revision

Formula Meaning
$$\oint H\cdot dl=I_{enc}$$ Ampère's Circuit Law
$$I_{enc}=\int J\cdot dS$$ Enclosed Current
