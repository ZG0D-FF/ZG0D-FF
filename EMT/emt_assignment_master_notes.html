Question 01 Time-Harmonic Electrodynamics

Maxwell's Four Equations in Phasor Form & Purpose of Phasor Formalism

1. Mention the Maxwell's four equations in the form of phasor. State the purpose of using phasor.

In linear, homogeneous, and isotropic (LHI) media excited by sinusoidal steady-state sources of frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$, every real instantaneous field vector $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z,t)$ relates to its spatial phasor $\mathbf{F}_s(x,y,z)$ via Euler's identity:

$$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z,t) = \operatorname{Re}\left[\mathbf{F}_s(x,y,z)\,e^{j\omega t}\right], \quad \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}}{\partial t} \longleftrightarrow j\omega\,\mathbf{F}_s$$
Physical Law Time-Domain Differential Phasor Point Form Phasor Integral Form
Gauss's Law (Electric) $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} = \rho_v$ $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D}_s = \rho_{vs} \iff \nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}_s = \frac{\rho_{vs}}{\varepsilon}$ $\oint_S \mathbf{D}_s \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_V \rho_{vs}\,dV$
Gauss's Law (Magnetic) $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$ $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B}_s = 0 \iff \nabla \cdot \mathbf{H}_s = 0$ $\oint_S \mathbf{B}_s \cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0$
Faraday's Induction Law $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$ $\nabla \times \mathbf{E}_s = -j\omega \mathbf{B}_s = -j\omega\mu \mathbf{H}_s$ $\oint_C \mathbf{E}_s \cdot d\mathbf{l} = -j\omega \int_S \mathbf{B}_s \cdot d\mathbf{S}$
Ampère-Maxwell Law $\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_c + \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$ $\nabla \times \mathbf{H}_s = \mathbf{J}_{cs} + j\omega \mathbf{D}_s = (\sigma + j\omega\varepsilon)\mathbf{E}_s$ $\oint_C \mathbf{H}_s \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S (\mathbf{J}_{cs} + j\omega\mathbf{D}_s) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$
💡 Lucid Understanding: The 4 Core Engineering Purposes of Phasors Expand
  1. Dimensionality Reduction: Eliminates the time variable $t$ completely, reducing 4-variable coupled PDEs $(x,y,z,t)$ into purely spatial 3-variable differential equations $(x,y,z)$.
  2. Algebraization of Calculus: Replaces continuous time differentiation $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$ with algebraic multiplication by $j\omega$, and integration $\int dt$ with algebraic division by $j\omega$. The 4D wave equation becomes the time-independent vector Helmholtz equation: $$\nabla^2 \mathbf{E}_s - \gamma^2 \mathbf{E}_s = 0, \quad \gamma = \sqrt{j\omega\mu(\sigma + j\omega\varepsilon)}$$
  3. Unification of Loss via Complex Permittivity: Combines ohmic conduction current $(\sigma \mathbf{E})$ and displacement current $(j\omega\varepsilon \mathbf{E})$ into a single unified term: $$\nabla \times \mathbf{H}_s = j\omega \left[\varepsilon - j\frac{\sigma}{\omega}\right]\mathbf{E}_s = j\omega \varepsilon^* \mathbf{E}_s$$
  4. Arbitrary Signal Synthesis via Fourier: By linearity, determining steady-state response $\mathbf{H}_s(j\omega)$ unlocks full arbitrary pulse/transient response through Inverse Fourier Transformation.

Question 03 Free Space Dynamics

Evaluation of Displacement Current Density $\mathbf{J}_d$ and Angular Frequency $\omega$

3. If the electric field propagating in free space is $\mathbf{E} = 30 \sin(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y\text{ V/m}$. Evaluate: (a) displacement current density $\mathbf{J}_d$ (b) frequency $\omega$.
Step 1 · Parameter Extraction & Propagation Direction

Given $\mathbf{E}(x,t) = E_0 \sin(\omega t - \beta x)\,\hat{a}_y$ propagating in free space ($\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0, \mu = \mu_0, \sigma = 0$):

$E_0 = 30\text{ V/m}, \quad \beta = 2\text{ rad/m}, \quad \hat{a}_k = +\hat{a}_x$
Step 2 · Part (b) Angular Frequency $\omega$ & Linear Frequency $f$

In free space, phase velocity equals the speed of light $u_p = c_0 = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}$:

$$\omega = \beta\,c_0 = 2\text{ rad/m} \times (3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}) = 6 \times 10^8\text{ rad/s} \quad (600\text{ Mrad/s})$$

Linear cyclical frequency: $f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{6 \times 10^8}{2\pi} \approx 95.493\text{ MHz}$ (FM broadcast band).
Exact CODATA 2018 value ($c_0 = 299,792,458\text{ m/s}$): $\omega = 5.995849 \times 10^8\text{ rad/s}, f = 95.4269\text{ MHz}$.

Step 3 · Part (a) Displacement Current Density $\mathbf{J}_d(x,t)$

By Maxwell's fourth equation, displacement current density is defined as the time rate of change of electric flux density $\mathbf{D} = \varepsilon_0\mathbf{E}$:

$$\mathbf{J}_d(x,t) = \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t} = \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} = \varepsilon_0 \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[30 \sin(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y\right] = 30\omega\varepsilon_0 \cos(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y$$

Using the analytical identity $\omega\varepsilon_0 = \frac{\beta}{\eta_0} = \frac{2}{120\pi} = \frac{1}{60\pi}\text{ S/m}$:

$$J_{d0} = 30 \times \frac{1}{60\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\text{ A/m}^2 \approx 0.159155\text{ A/m}^2 = 159.155\text{ mA/m}^2$$
FINAL ANSWER (Q3):
(a) $\mathbf{J}_d(x,t) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \cos(6\times 10^8 t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y \approx 0.1592 \cos(6\times 10^8 t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y\text{ A/m}^2$
(b) $\omega = 6 \times 10^8\text{ rad/s}$ ($f \approx 95.49\text{ MHz}$)
Question 04 Maxwell-Faraday & TEM Impedance

Determination of Magnetic Field Intensity $\mathbf{H}$ in the Electromagnetic Wave

4. In the previous problem determine the magnetic field $\mathbf{H}$ in the electromagnetic wave.
Method 1 · Direct Integration of Faraday's Curl Law

In source-free free space, $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\mu_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{H}}{\partial t}$. Since $\mathbf{E} = E_y(x,t)\,\hat{a}_y$:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x}\,\hat{a}_z = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[30 \sin(\omega t - 2x)\right]\hat{a}_z = -60 \cos(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z$$

Equating to $-\mu_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{H}}{\partial t}$ and integrating with respect to time:

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{H}}{\partial t} = \frac{60}{\mu_0} \cos(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z \implies \mathbf{H}(x,t) = \frac{60}{\omega\mu_0} \sin(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z$$

Substituting $\omega\mu_0 = (6\times 10^8)(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) = 240\pi\ \Omega/\text{m}$:

$$H_0 = \frac{60}{240\pi} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\text{ A/m} \approx 0.079577\text{ A/m} = 79.577\text{ mA/m}$$
Method 2 · Cross-Verification via TEM Intrinsic Impedance Cross Product

For any uniform plane wave propagating along unit vector $\hat{a}_k = +\hat{a}_x$ in free space ($\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$):

$$\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{\eta_0}(\hat{a}_k \times \mathbf{E}) = \frac{1}{120\pi}\left[\hat{a}_x \times 30\sin(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y\right] = \frac{30}{120\pi}\sin(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z = \frac{1}{4\pi}\sin(6\times 10^8 t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z\text{ A/m}$$

Curl check: $\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = -\frac{\partial H_z}{\partial x}\,\hat{a}_y = -\left[-\frac{2}{4\pi}\cos(\omega t - 2x)\right]\hat{a}_y = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cos(\omega t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_y \equiv \mathbf{J}_d(x,t)$ (Exact identity!).

FINAL ANSWER (Q4):
$\mathbf{H}(x,t) = \frac{1}{4\pi} \sin(6\times 10^8 t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z \approx 0.0796 \sin(6\times 10^8 t - 2x)\,\hat{a}_z\text{ A/m}$ ($79.58\text{ mA/m}$)
Question 05 Dissipative Media & Ferromagnetism

Evaluation of Loss Tangent, Complex Intrinsic Impedance $\eta_c$ & Medium Classification at 2 MHz

5. A medium for EM wave propagation is characterized by the following parameters: $\varepsilon_r = 2.5, \mu_r = 205, \sigma = 7\text{ S/m}$. Evaluate loss-tangent and intrinsic impedance of the medium at 2 MHz. Check whether the medium is lossy dielectric or good conductor.
Step 1 · Extract Parameters at $f = 2\text{ MHz}$
$f = 2 \times 10^6\text{ Hz} \implies \omega = 2\pi f = 4\pi \times 10^6 \approx 1.256637 \times 10^7\text{ rad/s}$
$\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r = 2.5 \times (8.854188 \times 10^{-12}) = 2.213547 \times 10^{-11}\text{ F/m}$
$\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r = 205 \times (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) = 8.2\pi \times 10^{-5}\text{ H/m} \approx 2.576106 \times 10^{-4}\text{ H/m}$
$\sigma = 7\text{ S/m}$
Step 2 · Loss Tangent Evaluation & Medium Classification
$$\tan \delta_c = \frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon} = \frac{7}{(4\pi \times 10^6)(2.213547 \times 10^{-11})} = \frac{7}{2.781625 \times 10^{-4}} = \mathbf{25,165.14} \approx 2.52 \times 10^4$$
Classification Verdict
Standard engineering boundary: $\tan \delta_c > 100 \implies$ Good Conductor.
Here $\tan \delta_c \approx 25,165 \gg 100$. Conduction current exceeds displacement current by four orders of magnitude! The medium behaves unequivocally as a Good Conductor (specifically, a conductive ferromagnetic material due to $\mu_r = 205$).
Step 3 · Complex Intrinsic Impedance $\eta_c$

For a good conductor ($\sigma \gg \omega\varepsilon$):

$$\eta_c \approx \sqrt{\frac{j\omega\mu}{\sigma}} = \sqrt{\frac{\omega\mu}{\sigma}}\,\angle 45^\circ = \sqrt{\frac{(4\pi \times 10^6)(205 \times 4\pi \times 10^{-7})}{7}}\,\angle 45^\circ = \sqrt{\frac{3237.2303}{7}}\,\angle 45^\circ = \sqrt{462.4615}\,\angle 45^\circ = \mathbf{21.505\,\angle 45^\circ\ \Omega}$$

In rectangular Cartesian form:

$$\eta_c = 21.5049 \left(\cos 45^\circ + j\sin 45^\circ\right) = \mathbf{15.206 + j\,15.206\ \Omega}$$
⚠️ University Examination Trap: Ferromagnetic $\mu_r = 205$
Students routinely assume non-magnetic medium ($\mu_r = 1$) and use $\mu_0$ alone! In this problem $\mu_r = 205$. Omitting $\mu_r$ underestimates $|\eta_c|$ by a factor of $\sqrt{205} \approx 14.32$ ($1.50\ \Omega$ vs the correct $21.505\ \Omega$), resulting in zero marks!
FINAL ANSWER (Q5):
1. Loss Tangent: $\tan \delta_c = 25,165.14$ ($2.52 \times 10^4$)
2. Medium Classification: Good Conductor (since $\tan \delta_c \gg 100$)
3. Intrinsic Impedance: $\eta_c = 21.505 \angle 45^\circ\ \Omega = (15.206 + j15.206)\ \Omega$
Question 06 d'Alembert Phase Invariance & VLF Band

Direction of Wave Propagation and Phase Constant $\beta$

6. The electric field in free space is $\mathbf{E} = 20 \sin(10^5 t + \beta x)\,\hat{a}_y\text{ V/m}$. Determine the (a) direction of wave propagation (b) phase constant ($\beta$).
Step 1 · Part (a) Direction of Wave Propagation

By d'Alembert's traveling wave theorem, a surface of constant instantaneous phase requires:

$$\Phi(x,t) = \omega t + \beta x = 10^5 t + \beta x = \text{constant}$$

Taking the total time derivative ($d\Phi/dt = 0$):

$$\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} = 0 \implies 10^5 + \beta \frac{dx}{dt} = 0 \implies u_p = \frac{dx}{dt} = -\frac{10^5}{\beta} < 0$$

Because $\omega > 0$ and $\beta > 0$, the wave crest moves toward decreasing $x$. Thus, the wave propagates along the negative x-direction ($-\hat{a}_x$).

Step 2 · Part (b) Phase Constant $\beta$ Evaluation

In lossless free space, phase constant $\beta$ relates to angular frequency $\omega = 10^5\text{ rad/s}$ by:

$$\beta = \frac{\omega}{c_0} = \frac{10^5\text{ rad/s}}{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}} = \frac{1}{3000}\text{ rad/m} = \frac{1}{3} \times 10^{-3}\text{ rad/m} \approx \mathbf{3.3333 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/m}}$$

Exact CODATA 2018 value: $\beta = \frac{10^5}{299792458} = 3.335641 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/m}$.
Physical Context: $f = \frac{10^5}{2\pi} \approx 15.915\text{ kHz}$ (Very Low Frequency VLF band). Free space wavelength is $\lambda_0 = \frac{2\pi}{\beta} = 6000\pi\text{ m} \approx 18.85\text{ km}$, widely used in naval submarine communications.

FINAL ANSWER (Q6):
(a) Direction of Wave Propagation: Negative x-direction ($-\hat{a}_x$)
(b) Phase Constant: $\beta = \frac{1}{3} \times 10^{-3}\text{ rad/m} \approx 3.333 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/m}$
Question 07 Faraday Induction & Bessel Diffusion

Mechanism of Eddy Current Crowding (Skin Effect) in a Solid Conducting Wire

7. With proper diagram show how eddy current restrict the flow of current near the surface in case of a solid conducting wire of finite thickness.

In a solid cylindrical wire of radius $a$ carrying AC current $I(t)$ along $+\hat{a}_z$, current density is not uniform across the cross-section. The physical restriction of current to the outer surface arises from the coupled action of Ampère's circuital law and Faraday's induction law.

Physical Mechanism Step Governing Maxwell Equation Electromagnetic Dynamic
1. Internal Azimuthal Flux $\oint \mathbf{H} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_{enc}$ Axial current $I_0 \hat{a}_z$ creates concentric azimuthal magnetic flux $\mathbf{B}_\phi(r,t)\,\hat{a}_\phi$ inside the wire ($0 < r < a$).
2. Time-Varying Flux Linkage $\frac{\partial B_\phi}{\partial t} \ne 0$ Any longitudinal $r-z$ plane is penetrated orthogonally by time-varying magnetic flux $\Phi_\phi(t)$.
3. Faraday-Lenz Induced Loops $\oint \mathbf{E}_{eddy} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_\phi}{dt}$ Induces circulating eddy current loops $\mathbf{J}_{eddy} = \sigma \mathbf{E}_{eddy}$ in every meridian $r-z$ plane.
4. Core Cancellation vs Surface Addition $\mathbf{J}_{total} = \mathbf{J}_0 + \mathbf{J}_{eddy}$ Near central axis ($r \to 0$): $\mathbf{J}_{eddy}$ flows backward ($-\hat{a}_z$) $\implies J_{net} = J_0 - J_{eddy}$ (Destructive cancellation).
Near surface ($r \to a$): $\mathbf{J}_{eddy}$ flows forward ($+\hat{a}_z$) $\implies J_{net} = J_0 + J_{eddy}$ (Constructive reinforcement).
💡 Lucid Understanding: Exact Cylindrical Bessel Derivation & Lord Kelvin Functions Expand Derivation

Combining $\nabla \times \mathbf{E}_s = -j\omega\mu\mathbf{H}_s$ and $\nabla \times \mathbf{H}_s = \sigma\mathbf{E}_s$ in cylindrical coordinates $(r,\phi,z)$ gives the vector Helmholtz diffusion equation for axial current $J_z(r)$: $$\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dJ_z}{dr}\right) - j\omega\mu\sigma J_z = 0$$ Defining skin depth $\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi f \mu \sigma}}$, the exact analytical solution across the radius is expressed via zero-order Bessel functions (Lord Kelvin's $\text{ber}$ and $\text{bei}$ functions): $$\frac{J_z(r)}{J_z(a)} = \frac{J_0\left(j^{3/2}\sqrt{2}\,\frac{r}{\delta}\right)}{J_0\left(j^{3/2}\sqrt{2}\,\frac{a}{\delta}\right)} = \frac{\operatorname{ber}(\sqrt{2}r/\delta) + j\operatorname{bei}(\sqrt{2}r/\delta)}{\operatorname{ber}(\sqrt{2}a/\delta) + j\operatorname{bei}(\sqrt{2}a/\delta)}$$ For wire radius $a \gg \delta$, this reduces to the planar asymptotic exponential decay in radial depth $d = a - r$: $$J_z(d) \approx J_{surface}\,e^{-d/\delta}\,e^{-j(d/\delta)}$$ At depth $d = \delta$, amplitude drops to $e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%$; at $d = 3\delta$, drops to $4.98\%$; at $d = 5\delta$, drops to $0.67\%$. AC resistance increases by $R_{AC}/R_{DC} \approx \frac{a}{2\delta}$.

Question 08 Dielectric Relaxation & Complex Poynting Theorem

Physical Significance of Complex Permittivity for Both Real and Imaginary Parts

8. Mention the significance of complex permittivity for both the real and imaginary parts.

In any real dissipative medium subjected to time-harmonic excitation $\mathbf{E}_s e^{j\omega t}$, total permittivity is defined as $\varepsilon^* = \varepsilon' - j\varepsilon'' = \varepsilon_0(\varepsilon_r' - j\varepsilon_r'')$. Substituting into the Ampère-Maxwell equation gives:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{H}_s = \sigma \mathbf{E}_s + j\omega(\varepsilon_d' - j\varepsilon_d'')\mathbf{E}_s = j\omega \left[\varepsilon_d' - j\left(\varepsilon_d'' + \frac{\sigma}{\omega}\right)\right]\mathbf{E}_s \equiv j\omega(\varepsilon' - j\varepsilon'')\mathbf{E}_s$$
Physical Characteristic Real Part: $\varepsilon' = \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r'$ (Storage) Imaginary Part: $\varepsilon'' = \varepsilon_d'' + \frac{\sigma}{\omega}$ (Loss)
Fundamental Role Quantifies reversible electrostatic potential energy storage via bound polarization. Quantifies irreversible dissipation of electromagnetic energy into heat (Joule + dipolar loss).
Poynting Energy Relation Time-average stored electric energy density:
$\langle w_e \rangle = \frac{1}{4}\varepsilon' |\mathbf{E}_s|^2\text{ (J/m}^3\text{)}$		 Time-average power dissipated per unit volume:
$\langle p_d \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\omega\varepsilon'' |\mathbf{E}_s|^2\text{ (W/m}^3\text{)}$
Current Phase Quadrature reactive current $j\omega\varepsilon'\mathbf{E}_s$ leading $\mathbf{E}_s$ by $90^\circ$ (Zero power loss). In-phase dissipative current $\omega\varepsilon''\mathbf{E}_s$ at $0^\circ$ with $\mathbf{E}_s$ (Maximum active absorption).
Wave Propagation Governs phase constant $\beta \approx \omega\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon'}$, phase velocity $u_p \approx c_0/\sqrt{\varepsilon_r'}$, and refractive index $n \approx \sqrt{\varepsilon_r'}$. Directly governs attenuation constant $\alpha$ and skin depth $\delta = 1/\alpha$. If $\varepsilon'' = 0 \implies \alpha = 0$ (No loss).
Circuit Analogy Shunt capacitance $C = \varepsilon'\frac{A}{d}$. Parallel loss conductance $G = \omega\varepsilon''\frac{A}{d}$, loss tangent $\tan \delta = \frac{\varepsilon''}{\varepsilon'} = \frac{1}{Q_d}$.
Kramers-Kronig Coupling
By causality (polarization cannot precede the applied electric field), $\varepsilon'(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon''(\omega)$ are linked by Hilbert transformation. Any medium exhibiting frequency dispersion in storage $\varepsilon'(\omega)$ must inevitably exhibit non-zero absorption $\varepsilon''(\omega)$.
Question 09 UHF Intermediate Dispersive Regime

Evaluation of Attenuation Constant $\alpha$ and Phase Constant $\beta$ at 400 MHz

9. Suppose an electromagnetic signal at 400 MHz is propagating through a medium with the parameters such as $\varepsilon_r = 50, \mu_r = 1, \sigma = 5\text{ S/m}$. Evaluate (i) attenuation constant ($\alpha$) (ii) phase constant ($\beta$).
CRITICAL EXAMINATION DIAGNOSTIC: THE ORDER-OF-UNITY TRAP!
Let us first compute the loss tangent before picking any formula: $$\omega = 2\pi(400 \times 10^6) = 8\pi \times 10^8 \approx 2.513274 \times 10^9\text{ rad/s}$$ $$\omega\varepsilon = (8\pi \times 10^8) \times 50 \times \left(\frac{10^{-9}}{36\pi}\right) = \frac{40}{36} = \frac{10}{9} \approx 1.1111\text{ S/m}$$ $$\tan \delta_c = \frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon} = \frac{5}{10/9} = \mathbf{4.5000}$$ Because $\tan \delta_c = 4.5$ is of order unity ($0.01 < \tan \delta_c < 100$), NEITHER the low-loss dielectric formula ($\tan \delta_c \ll 1$) nor the good conductor approximation ($\alpha \approx \beta \approx \sqrt{\pi f \mu \sigma} = 88.86$) is valid! Using good conductor formulas causes an error of $+11.7\%$ for $\alpha$ and $-10.4\%$ for $\beta$! Exact general Helmholtz formulas must be used.
Step 1 · Exact General Propagation Formulas
$$\alpha = \omega \sqrt{\frac{\mu\varepsilon}{2}\left[\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon}\right)^2} - 1\right]}, \quad \beta = \omega \sqrt{\frac{\mu\varepsilon}{2}\left[\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon}\right)^2} + 1\right]}$$
Step 2 · Prefactor & Radical Evaluation

1. Common prefactor $\frac{\omega^2\mu\varepsilon}{2}$ (using $c_0 = 3\times 10^8\text{ m/s}, \varepsilon_0 = \frac{10^{-9}}{36\pi}$):

$$\omega\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon} = \frac{\omega}{c_0}\sqrt{\varepsilon_r} = \frac{8\pi \times 10^8}{3 \times 10^8}\sqrt{50} = \frac{8\pi\sqrt{50}}{3} \approx 59.23844\text{ rad/m}$$ $$\frac{\omega^2\mu\varepsilon}{2} = \frac{(59.23844)^2}{2} = \mathbf{1754.5964\text{ m}^{-2}}$$

2. Dimensionless radical term with $\tan \delta_c = 4.5$:

$$\sqrt{1 + (\tan \delta_c)^2} = \sqrt{1 + (4.5)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 20.25} = \sqrt{21.25} \approx \mathbf{4.609772}$$
Step 3 · Numerical Computation of $\alpha$ and $\beta$

(i) Attenuation Constant $\alpha$:

$$\alpha = \sqrt{1754.5964 \times (4.609772 - 1)} = \sqrt{1754.5964 \times 3.609772} = \sqrt{6333.694} = \mathbf{79.5845\text{ Np/m}} \approx \mathbf{79.585\text{ Np/m}}$$ $$\alpha_{dB} = 8.68589 \times 79.5845 = \mathbf{691.26\text{ dB/m}}$$

(ii) Phase Constant $\beta$:

$$\beta = \sqrt{1754.5964 \times (4.609772 + 1)} = \sqrt{1754.5964 \times 5.609772} = \sqrt{9842.887} = \mathbf{99.2113\text{ rad/m}} \approx \mathbf{99.211\text{ rad/m}}$$

Exact CODATA 2018 value: $\alpha = 79.573\text{ Np/m}, \beta = 99.226\text{ rad/m}$.
Cross-check via $2\alpha\beta = \omega\mu\sigma$: $2 \times 79.5845 \times 99.2113 = 15,791.36\text{ m}^{-2}$, while $\omega\mu\sigma = (8\pi \times 10^8)(4\pi \times 10^{-7})(5) = 16\pi^2 \times 100 \approx 15,791.37\text{ m}^{-2}$ (Exact match!).

FINAL ANSWER (Q9):
(i) Attenuation Constant: $\alpha = 79.585\text{ Np/m}$ (CODATA: $79.573\text{ Np/m}$; $691.2\text{ dB/m}$)
(ii) Phase Constant: $\beta = 99.211\text{ rad/m}$ (CODATA: $99.226\text{ rad/m}$)
Question 10 Wavelength Compression & Biomedical MICS

Evaluation of Wavelength $\lambda$, Skin Depth $\delta$ and Medium Identification

10. For the previous problem determine the (i) wavelength of the signal in the medium (ii) skin depth ($\delta$) in the medium. Thereafter mention which type of medium it is?
Step 1 · Part (i) Wavelength in the Medium $\lambda$

Using the exact phase constant $\beta = 99.2113\text{ rad/m}$ from Question 9:

$$\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{\beta} = \frac{2\pi}{99.2113\text{ rad/m}} = \mathbf{0.063331\text{ m}} = \mathbf{6.333\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{63.331\text{ mm}}$$

Comparison with Free Space: $\lambda_0 = \frac{c_0}{f} = \frac{3\times 10^8}{400\times 10^6} = 0.75\text{ m} = 75\text{ cm}$. Inside this high-permittivity conductive medium, the wavelength is compressed by a factor of $\frac{75}{6.333} \approx 11.84$! Phase velocity drops to $u_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = 2.533 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}$ (only $8.45\%$ of $c_0$).

Step 2 · Part (ii) Skin Depth / Penetration Depth $\delta$

By standard IEEE definition, skin depth $\delta$ is the distance over which fields attenuate by a factor of $e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%$ ($\delta = 1/\alpha$):

$$\delta = \frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{79.5845\text{ Np/m}} = \mathbf{0.012565\text{ m}} = \mathbf{1.257\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{12.565\text{ mm}}$$
⚠️ Blind Good-Conductor Formula Warning
If one naively uses $\delta_{approx} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi f \mu \sigma}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(4\times 10^8)(4\pi \times 10^{-7})(5)}} = \frac{1}{20\sqrt{2}\pi} \approx 0.01125\text{ m} = 1.125\text{ cm}$, it underestimates penetration depth by $10.4\%$! Displacement current is $18.2\%$ of conduction current and cannot be neglected. $\delta = 1.257\text{ cm}$ is the true physical skin depth.
Step 3 · Part (iii) Medium Classification & Physical Identification

1. Theoretical Electromagnetic Classification: Since $\tan \delta_c = 4.5$ lies strictly in the intermediate regime $0.01 < \tan \delta_c < 100$, this medium is classified as a Lossy Dielectric (or quasi-conductor / moderately conductive dissipative dielectric).

2. Real-World Physical Identification: A non-magnetic medium ($\mu_r = 1$) at 400 MHz with high dielectric constant $\varepsilon_r = 50$ (arising from water dipole orientation) and ionic conductivity $\sigma = 5\text{ S/m}$ directly represents saline water / seawater ($\varepsilon_r \approx 70-80, \sigma \approx 4-5\text{ S/m}$) and high-water-content human biological tissue (skeletal muscle, blood, or saline phantom) operating in the 401–406 MHz Medical Implant Communication Service (MICS) band.

FINAL ANSWER (Q10):
(i) Wavelength: $\lambda = 0.06333\text{ m} = 6.333\text{ cm}$ ($63.33\text{ mm}$)
(ii) Skin Depth: $\delta = 1/\alpha = 0.01257\text{ m} = 1.257\text{ cm}$ ($12.57\text{ mm}$)
(iii) Medium Type: Lossy Dielectric (Quasi-Conductor); physically representative of saline water / human muscle biological phantom at 400 MHz (MICS band).

Trap 1 (Q3 & Q6) Assuming $\beta x$ always means $+x$ propagation. Check relative signs: $\sin(\omega t - \beta x)$ travels along $+x$, while $\sin(\omega t + \beta x)$ travels along $-x$ because phase invariance requires $\frac{dx}{dt} = -\frac{\omega}{\beta} < 0$.
Trap 2 (Q5 & Q9) Blindly applying good-conductor formulas without checking $\tan \delta_c$. Always compute $\tan \delta_c = \frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon}$ first! In Q5, $\tan \delta_c \approx 25,165 \gg 100 \implies$ Good conductor. In Q9, $\tan \delta_c = 4.5 \implies$ Exact formulas mandatory!
