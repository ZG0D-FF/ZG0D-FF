1. Principle of Charge Conservation

Due to the principle of charge conservation, the time rate of decrease of charge within a given volume must equal the net outward current flow through the closed surface of the volume.

Current coming out of a closed surface is:

$$\oint \mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=-\frac{dQ_{in}}{dt}$$
  • J – current density
  • $Q_{in}$ – total charge enclosed within the closed surface

2. Derivation of the Continuity Equation

The enclosed charge is expressed as:

$$Q=\int_V\rho_v\,dV$$

Substituting into the charge conservation equation:

$$\oint \mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_V\rho_v\,dV$$

Using the divergence theorem:

$$\int_V(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J})\,dV=-\int_V\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial t}\,dV$$

Since both integrals are over the same arbitrary volume:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}=-\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial t}$$

This is the continuity equation.

3. Physical Meaning

The continuity equation states that:

Current leaves

The charge stored inside decreases.

Current enters

The stored charge increases.

Conservation

Charge can neither be created nor destroyed.

4. Relaxation Time

Inside a conducting material, use:

Ohm's law

$$\mathbf{J}=\sigma\mathbf{E}$$

Gauss's law

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}=\frac{\rho_v}{\varepsilon}$$

Substitute both into the continuity equation:

$$\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial t}+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\rho_v=0$$

This is a homogeneous linear ordinary differential equation.

5. Solution of the Differential Equation

Separate variables:

$$\frac{d\rho_v}{\rho_v}=-\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\,dt$$

Integrating both sides:

$$\ln\rho_v=-\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}t+C$$

Therefore, the charge density becomes:

$$\rho_v(t)=\rho_0\,e^{-\sigma t/\varepsilon}$$
  • $\rho_0$ – initial charge density
  • σ – conductivity
  • ε – permittivity

6. Relaxation Time Constant

The relaxation time is:

$$\tau=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}$$

Hence, the charge density can also be written as:

$$\rho_v(t)=\rho_0\,e^{-t/\tau}$$

Relaxation time represents how quickly excess charge disappears inside a material.

7. Example: Copper

Given:

  • Conductivity: $\sigma=5.8\times10^{7}\ \text{S/m}$
  • Relative permittivity: $\varepsilon_r=1$, so $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_0\approx8.854\times10^{-12}\ \text{F/m}$
$$\tau=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}\approx1.53\times10^{-19}\ \text{s}$$

This extremely small relaxation time means:

Charge disappears almost instantly from the interior.

It quickly moves to the surface.

Good conductors cannot retain excess charge inside.

10. Two-Mark Exam Points

State the continuity equation

The continuity equation is

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}=-\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial t}$$

It expresses the conservation of electric charge.

Define relaxation time

Relaxation time is the time required for excess charge inside a material to decay exponentially.

$$\tau=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}$$

Why is relaxation time very small for conductors?

Conductors have very high conductivity, so

$$\tau=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}$$

becomes extremely small, causing charge to move rapidly to the surface.
