Ampère's Circuit Law

The circulation of H around any closed path equals the current it encloses.

Definition

Ampère's circuit law states that the line integral of H around a closed path equals the net current enclosed by the path.

$$\oint_L\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=I_{enc}$$
  • H – magnetic field intensity
  • dl – differential length element
  • $I_{enc}$ – enclosed current

Important Notes

Like Gauss's law, Ampère's law is easiest to apply when the current distribution is symmetrical.

The equation is always valid, but it is mainly used to find H when symmetry exists.

It can be derived from the Biot–Savart law.

Maxwell's Third Equation

Applying Stokes' theorem:

$$I_{enc}=\oint_L\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=\int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Also,

$$I_{enc}=\int_S\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Comparing both gives

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}$$

This is the third Maxwell's equation in differential form. Since $\nabla\times\mathbf{H}\neq0$, the magnetostatic field is not conservative.

Applications

Used for symmetrical current distributions.

Infinite line current

H circles the wire, with $H=\dfrac{I}{2\pi r}$.

Infinite sheet current

H is uniform and parallel to the sheet on each side.

Coaxial line

Field exists only between the conductors.

For symmetrical distributions, H is either parallel or perpendicular to dl. Where H is parallel to dl, its magnitude is constant.
