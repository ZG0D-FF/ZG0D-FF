Quick Overview

This chapter explains how a changing magnetic field produces an electric field, introduces transformer and motional EMF, explains Maxwell's displacement current, and presents Maxwell's four equations.

9.2 Faraday's Law

Historical background

After Oersted showed that electric current produces a magnetic field, Michael Faraday and Joseph Henry discovered that a time-varying magnetic field can produce electricity.

Main idea

  • A static magnetic field does not produce current.
  • A changing magnetic field induces an electromotive force (EMF).
  • The induced EMF causes current to flow in a closed circuit.

Faraday's law

$$V_{emf}=-\frac{d\lambda}{dt}=-N\frac{d\psi}{dt}$$
SymbolMeaning
$V_{emf}$Induced EMF (volts)
$\lambda$Flux linkage
$N$Number of turns
$\psi$Magnetic flux

Lenz's law: the induced current always flows in a direction that opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.

Magnetic flux

$$\psi=\int\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Flux depends upon magnetic field, surface area and angle.

Electric field produced by a changing magnetic field

$$\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=-\frac{d}{dt}\int\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

A changing magnetic field creates a circulating electric field even without a conducting wire.

9.3 Transformer and Motional EMF

Transformer EMF

Produced when the conductor remains stationary while the magnetic field changes.

$$\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=-\frac{d}{dt}\int\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Using Stokes' theorem:

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

Motional EMF

Produced when the conductor moves through a magnetic field.

Magnetic force:

$$\mathbf{F}=q(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B})$$

General EMF:

$$V_{emf}=\int(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{l}$$

For a straight conductor, with $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{B}$ and the conductor mutually perpendicular:

$$V_{emf}=uBL$$

9.4 Displacement Current

Ampère's original law could not explain current continuity inside a capacitor. Maxwell introduced displacement current to solve this problem.

Original law:

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}$$

Displacement current density:

$$\mathbf{J}_d=\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Modified Ampère's law:

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Inside a capacitor, conduction current does not cross the dielectric, but displacement current exists and maintains current continuity.

9.5 Maxwell's Equations

James Clerk Maxwell unified electricity and magnetism into one theory. These four equations form the foundation of electromagnetics.

1. Gauss's law

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D}=\rho_v$$

2. Gauss's law for magnetism

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$$

3. Faraday's law

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

4. Ampère–Maxwell law

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Viva & Exam One-Liners

Who discovered electromagnetic induction?Michael Faraday.
State Faraday's law.The induced EMF equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.
What does the negative sign indicate?Lenz's law.
Difference between transformer and motional EMF?Transformer EMF is caused by changing magnetic fields; motional EMF is caused by conductor motion.
Why did Maxwell introduce displacement current?To make Ampère's law valid for time-varying fields and explain electromagnetic wave propagation.
Which Maxwell equation represents Faraday's law?$\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$
