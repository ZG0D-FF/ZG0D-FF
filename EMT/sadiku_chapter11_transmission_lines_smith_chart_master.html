Section 11.2

Transmission Line Distributed Parameters & Table 11.1

A transmission line operates in the distributed regime when physical line length $\ell \ge \lambda / 20$. Under this condition, lumped Kirchoff laws break down because wave propagation transit time cannot be neglected. The electrical behavior is governed by 4 distributed parameters per unit length: Resistance $R$ ($\Omega/\text{m}$), Inductance $L$ ($\text{H/m}$), Conductance $G$ ($\text{S/m}$), and Capacitance $C$ ($\text{F/m}$).

Line Geometry Resistance $R$ ($\Omega/\text{m}$) Inductance $L$ ($\text{H/m}$) Conductance $G$ ($\text{S/m}$) Capacitance $C$ ($\text{F/m}$)
Coaxial Line
Inner $a$, Outer $b$, $\delta \ll a, b$		 $$\frac{1}{2\pi \delta \sigma_c}\left[\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}\right]$$ $$\frac{\mu}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$$ $$\frac{2\pi \sigma}{\ln(b/a)}$$ $$\frac{2\pi \varepsilon}{\ln(b/a)}$$
Two-Wire Line
Radius $a$, Spacing $d \gg a$		 $$\frac{1}{\pi a \delta \sigma_c}$$ $$\frac{\mu}{\pi}\cosh^{-1}\left(\frac{d}{2a}\right) \approx \frac{\mu}{\pi}\ln\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)$$ $$\frac{\pi \sigma}{\cosh^{-1}(d/2a)}$$ $$\frac{\pi \varepsilon}{\cosh^{-1}(d/2a)}$$
Planar Line
Width $w \gg d$, Thickness $t \gg \delta$		 $$\frac{2}{w \delta \sigma_c}$$ $$\frac{\mu d}{w}$$ $$\frac{\sigma w}{d}$$ $$\frac{\varepsilon w}{d}$$
Fundamental Universal Constitutive Identities (Holding for Any Uniform Two-Conductor Line): $$LC = \mu \varepsilon, \qquad \frac{G}{C} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}$$ $$\delta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi f \mu_c \sigma_c}} \quad (\text{Skin Depth of Conductor in meters})$$
💡 Lucid Understanding: Physical Meaning of $G \ne 1/R$ and Skin Depth

1. The Conductance $G$ is NOT the inverse of Resistance $R$: $R$ is the AC series ohmic resistance of the metallic conductors carrying longitudinal current $I_z$. In contrast, $G$ is the transverse leakage conductance through the imperfect dielectric medium separating the two conductors. A perfect dielectric has $\sigma = 0 \implies G = 0$, regardless of conductor resistance $R$.

2. External Inductance Dominance: The inductance $L$ in Table 11.1 represents external inductance $L_{\text{ext}}$. The internal inductance $L_{\text{in}} = R / \omega$ is confined within the skin depth and drops inversely with frequency, becoming negligible above UHF frequencies ($> 100\text{ MHz}$).

Section 11.3

Complete Derivation of Telegrapher's Equations & Helmholtz Uncoupling

Applying Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) around the differential outer loop of length $\Delta z$ and Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) at the central shunt node:

Step 1: KVL Outer Mesh Equation: $$V(z, t) - R\,\Delta z\,I(z, t) - L\,\Delta z\,\frac{\partial I(z, t)}{\partial t} - V(z + \Delta z, t) = 0$$ $$\lim_{\Delta z \to 0} -\frac{V(z+\Delta z, t) - V(z, t)}{\Delta z} = R\,I(z, t) + L\,\frac{\partial I(z, t)}{\partial t} \implies -\frac{\partial V(z, t)}{\partial z} = R\,I(z, t) + L\,\frac{\partial I(z, t)}{\partial t}$$
Step 2: KCL Shunt Node Equation: $$I(z, t) - I(z + \Delta z, t) = \Delta I = G\,\Delta z\,V(z + \Delta z, t) + C\,\Delta z\,\frac{\partial V(z + \Delta z, t)}{\partial t}$$ $$\lim_{\Delta z \to 0} -\frac{I(z+\Delta z, t) - I(z, t)}{\Delta z} = G\,V(z, t) + C\,\frac{\partial V(z, t)}{\partial t} \implies -\frac{\partial I(z, t)}{\partial z} = G\,V(z, t) + C\,\frac{\partial V(z, t)}{\partial t}$$
Step 3: Time-Harmonic Phasor Representation ($V(z,t) = \text{Re}[V_s(z)e^{j\omega t}]$): $$-\frac{dV_s}{dz} = (R + j\omega L) I_s, \qquad -\frac{dI_s}{dz} = (G + j\omega C) V_s$$
Step 4: Uncoupling to One-Dimensional Wave (Helmholtz) Equations: $$\frac{d^2 V_s}{dz^2} = -(R + j\omega L)\frac{dI_s}{dz} = (R + j\omega L)(G + j\omega C) V_s \implies \frac{d^2 V_s}{dz^2} - \gamma^2 V_s = 0$$ $$\frac{d^2 I_s}{dz^2} = -(G + j\omega C)\frac{dV_s}{dz} = (R + j\omega L)(G + j\omega C) I_s \implies \frac{d^2 I_s}{dz^2} - \gamma^2 I_s = 0$$ $$\gamma = \alpha + j\beta = \sqrt{(R + j\omega L)(G + j\omega C)}$$

The general forward ($+z$) and backward ($-z$) traveling wave solutions are:

$$V_s(z) = V_0^+ e^{-\gamma z} + V_0^- e^{\gamma z}, \qquad I_s(z) = I_0^+ e^{-\gamma z} + I_0^- e^{\gamma z} = \frac{V_0^+}{Z_0}e^{-\gamma z} - \frac{V_0^-}{Z_0}e^{\gamma z}$$ $$Z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{R + j\omega L}{G + j\omega C}} = R_0 + jX_0 \quad (\text{Characteristic Impedance in }\Omega)$$
Section 11.3 Special Cases

Lossless vs. Heaviside Distortionless Transmission Lines

Signals propagate as a superposition of many Fourier frequency components. Unless the attenuation constant $\alpha$ and the phase velocity $u$ are independent of frequency, signal pulses suffer geometric spreading and phase dispersion.

Property General Lossy Line Lossless Line ($R = 0 = G$) Distortionless Line ($RC = GL$)
Physical Condition $\sigma_c < \infty, \sigma > 0$ Perfect conductors ($\sigma_c \to \infty$), perfect dielectric ($\sigma = 0$) Balanced loss ratio: $R/L = G/C$ (Oliver Heaviside, 1887)
Attenuation $\alpha$ $\text{Re}\sqrt{(R+j\omega L)(G+j\omega C)}$ $$\alpha = 0$$ $$\alpha = \sqrt{RG} \quad (\text{Frequency Independent!})$$
Phase Constant $\beta$ $\text{Im}\sqrt{(R+j\omega L)(G+j\omega C)}$ $$\beta = \omega\sqrt{LC}$$ $$\beta = \omega\sqrt{LC} \quad (\text{Purely Linear in }\omega)$$
Phase Velocity $u$ $\omega / \beta$ (dispersive) $$u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{c}{\sqrt{\mu_r \varepsilon_r}}$$ $$u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \quad (\text{Zero Dispersion!})$$
Characteristic $Z_0$ $\sqrt{\frac{R+j\omega L}{G+j\omega C}} = R_0 + jX_0$ $$Z_0 = R_0 = \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}, \quad X_0 = 0$$ $$Z_0 = R_0 = \sqrt{\frac{R}{G}} = \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}, \quad X_0 = 0$$
Mathematical Proof of the Heaviside Distortionless Identity ($RC = GL$): $$\gamma = \sqrt{(R+j\omega L)(G+j\omega C)} = \sqrt{RG\left(1 + \frac{j\omega L}{R}\right)\left(1 + \frac{j\omega C}{G}\right)}$$ $$\text{Since } \frac{R}{L} = \frac{G}{C} \implies \frac{L}{R} = \frac{C}{G}, \quad \gamma = \sqrt{RG}\left(1 + \frac{j\omega C}{G}\right) = \sqrt{RG} + j\omega \frac{C\sqrt{RG}}{G} = \sqrt{RG} + j\omega \sqrt{LC}$$ $$\therefore \alpha = \sqrt{RG}, \qquad \beta = \omega\sqrt{LC} \quad \blacksquare$$
Section 11.4

Input Impedance $Z_{in}(\ell)$, Reflection Coefficient $\Gamma_L$ & VSWR ($s$)

Let a line of length $\ell$ with characteristic impedance $Z_0$ and propagation constant $\gamma$ be terminated by load impedance $Z_L$ at $z = \ell$. Taking the load as spatial origin $z' = \ell - z$:

General Lossy Line Input Impedance: $$Z_{in}(\ell) = \frac{V_s(z'=\ell)}{I_s(z'=\ell)} = Z_0 \left[\frac{Z_L + Z_0 \tanh \gamma \ell}{Z_0 + Z_L \tanh \gamma \ell}\right]$$ $$\text{For a Lossless Line } (\alpha = 0 \implies \gamma = j\beta, \tanh j\beta \ell = j\tan \beta \ell):$$ $$Z_{in}(\ell) = Z_0 \left[\frac{Z_L + j Z_0 \tan \beta \ell}{Z_0 + j Z_L \tan \beta \ell}\right]$$
Voltage Reflection Coefficient at the Load ($\Gamma_L$) and Along the Line ($\Gamma(z')$): $$\Gamma_L = \frac{V_0^- e^{\gamma \ell}}{V_0^+ e^{-\gamma \ell}} = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} = |\Gamma_L| e^{j\theta_\Gamma}$$ $$\Gamma(z') = \Gamma_L e^{-2\gamma z'} \xrightarrow{\text{lossless}} \Gamma_L e^{-j 2\beta z'}$$ $$\text{Standing Wave Ratio (SWR or } s \text{)}: \quad s = \frac{V_{\max}}{V_{\min}} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma_L|}{1 - |\Gamma_L|}, \qquad |\Gamma_L| = \frac{s - 1}{s + 1}$$ $$\text{Extremal Input Impedances: } \quad |Z_{in}|_{\max} = s Z_0, \qquad |Z_{in}|_{\min} = \frac{Z_0}{s}$$

Section 11.5 Conformal Mapping

The Smith Chart: Conformal Bilinear Transformation & Analytical Circle Proofs

Invented by Phillip H. Smith in 1939, the Smith chart is a graphical polar nomogram representing the conformal bilinear mapping between the complex normalized impedance plane $z_L = r + jx$ and the complex reflection coefficient plane $\Gamma = \Gamma_r + j\Gamma_i$.

Mapping Equation: $$\Gamma = \Gamma_r + j\Gamma_i = \frac{z_L - 1}{z_L + 1} = \frac{(r - 1) + jx}{(r + 1) + jx}$$ $$\text{Inverting for Normalized Impedance: } \quad z_L = r + jx = \frac{1 + \Gamma}{1 - \Gamma} = \frac{(1 + \Gamma_r) + j\Gamma_i}{(1 - \Gamma_r) - j\Gamma_i}$$
Step 1: Multiplying by Complex Conjugate $(1 - \Gamma_r) + j\Gamma_i$: $$z_L = \frac{[(1 + \Gamma_r) + j\Gamma_i][(1 - \Gamma_r) + j\Gamma_i]}{(1 - \Gamma_r)^2 + \Gamma_i^2} = \frac{(1 - \Gamma_r^2 - \Gamma_i^2) + j(2\Gamma_i)}{(1 - \Gamma_r)^2 + \Gamma_i^2}$$ $$\text{Equating Real Parts: } \quad r = \frac{1 - \Gamma_r^2 - \Gamma_i^2}{(1 - \Gamma_r)^2 + \Gamma_i^2}$$ $$\text{Equating Imaginary Parts: } \quad x = \frac{2\Gamma_i}{(1 - \Gamma_r)^2 + \Gamma_i^2}$$
Step 2: Rigorous Derivation of Constant Resistance ($r$) Circles: $$r\left[(1 - \Gamma_r)^2 + \Gamma_i^2\right] = 1 - \Gamma_r^2 - \Gamma_i^2 \implies r(1 - 2\Gamma_r + \Gamma_r^2 + \Gamma_i^2) = 1 - \Gamma_r^2 - \Gamma_i^2$$ $$\Gamma_r^2(1 + r) - 2r\Gamma_r + \Gamma_i^2(1 + r) = 1 - r \implies \Gamma_r^2 - \frac{2r}{1+r}\Gamma_r + \Gamma_i^2 = \frac{1 - r}{1 + r}$$ $$\text{Completing the square in }\Gamma_r: \quad \left(\Gamma_r - \frac{r}{1+r}\right)^2 + \Gamma_i^2 = \frac{1 - r}{1 + r} + \left(\frac{r}{1+r}\right)^2 = \frac{(1-r)(1+r) + r^2}{(1+r)^2} = \left(\frac{1}{1+r}\right)^2$$ $$\bbox[5px, border:2px solid #0071e3]{\left[\Gamma_r - \frac{r}{1+r}\right]^2 + \Gamma_i^2 = \left[\frac{1}{1+r}\right]^2}$$ $$\text{Circle Center: } \left(\frac{r}{1+r}, 0\right), \qquad \text{Radius: } \frac{1}{1+r}$$
Step 3: Rigorous Derivation of Constant Reactance ($x$) Circles: $$(1 - \Gamma_r)^2 + \Gamma_i^2 = \frac{2\Gamma_i}{x} \implies (\Gamma_r - 1)^2 + \Gamma_i^2 - \frac{2}{x}\Gamma_i = 0$$ $$\text{Completing the square in }\Gamma_i: \quad (\Gamma_r - 1)^2 + \left(\Gamma_i - \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^2$$ $$\bbox[5px, border:2px solid #0071e3]{(\Gamma_r - 1)^2 + \left[\Gamma_i - \frac{1}{x}\right]^2 = \left[\frac{1}{x}\right]^2}$$ $$\text{Circle Center: } \left(1, \frac{1}{x}\right), \qquad \text{Radius: } \frac{1}{|x|}$$

$r = 0$ (Boundary) $(0, 0)$ $1.0$ (Unit Circle) $x = 0$ (Real Axis) $(1, \infty)$ $\infty$ (Straight Line)

Critical Geometric Invariance: Singular Concurrence Point $(1,0)$
Every single $r$-circle and every single $x$-circle without exception passes through the point $(1, 0)$ in the complex $\Gamma$ plane. This point represents $Z_L = \infty$ (Open Circuit).
Section 11.5 Navigation

Smith Chart Rules of Navigation & The Admittance Dual

Key Navigational Laws:
  • Angular Equivalence: One complete $360^\circ$ mechanical rotation on the Smith chart corresponds to an electrical distance of $\lambda / 2$ along the physical transmission line: $$\lambda \longleftrightarrow 720^\circ, \qquad \frac{\lambda}{2} \longleftrightarrow 360^\circ, \qquad \frac{\lambda}{4} \longleftrightarrow 180^\circ$$
  • Direction of Rotation:
    • Clockwise (CW): Moving Toward the Generator (WTG) / Away from load.
    • Counter-Clockwise (CCW): Moving Toward the Load (WTL) / Away from generator.
  • Constant $s$ Circle: Concentric circle centered at the origin $(0, 0)$ with radius $|\Gamma_L|$. Where it crosses the right-hand horizontal axis ($\Gamma_r > 0$) gives the numerical value of VSWR $s = r_{\max}$. Where it crosses the left-hand axis gives $1/s = r_{\min}$.
  • $V_{\max}$ and $V_{\min}$ Locations: $V_{\max}$ (voltage antinode) is located at the right horizontal axis ($\theta_\Gamma = 0^\circ$). $V_{\min}$ (voltage node) is located at the left horizontal axis ($\theta_\Gamma = 180^\circ$). They are spaced exactly $\lambda / 4$ ($180^\circ$) apart.
  • Admittance Transformation: Normalized admittance $y = Y / Y_0 = g + jb$ is found diametrically opposite $z$ across the origin on the constant $s$-circle ($180^\circ$ rotation, corresponding to $\lambda/4$ shift). On the admittance chart, $r$-circles become conductance ($g$) circles and $x$-circles become susceptance ($b$) circles.
Section 11.6 Matching

Impedance Matching: Quarter-Wave Transformers & Single-Stub Shunt Tuners

Reflected waves cause standing waves, reducing power transmission and inducing high dielectric breakdown voltages. Two industry-standard matching mechanisms resolve this:

1. Quarter-Wave ($\lambda/4$) Transformer Matching: $$Z_0' = \sqrt{Z_0 Z_L} \quad (\text{Valid when } Z_L \text{ is purely real})$$ $$\text{Standing wave is completely eliminated to the left of the transformer: } s = 1.0, \quad \Gamma = 0$$ Disadvantage: Narrowband frequency sensitivity.
2. Single-Stub Shunt Tuner Matching (Short-Circuited): $$\text{Design Goal: Find distance } \ell \text{ from load where } y_{in}(\ell) = 1 + jb, \text{ then add shunt stub of length } d \text{ with } y_s = -jb$$ $$y_{\text{total}} = y_{in} + y_s = (1 + jb) + (-jb) = 1 + j0 \implies Z_{in} = Z_0$$ $$\text{Shorted Stub Input Admittance: } \quad y_s = \frac{Y_{sc}}{Y_0} = -j\cot \beta d$$ $$\text{Two matching locations exist per half-wavelength: } \ell_A \text{ and } \ell_B; \quad \text{Stub lengths: } d_A + d_B = \frac{\lambda}{2} = 0.5\lambda$$
💡 Lucid Understanding: Why Shorted Stubs are Preferred over Open Stubs

At microwave frequencies, an open-circuited transmission line stub acts as an efficient antenna, radiating significant RF power from its open unshielded tip into free space. This radiation loss creates stray coupling, degrades system $Q$, and detunes the matching network. In contrast, short-circuited stubs radiate virtually zero power and can be mechanically tuned with a sliding metallic shorting plunger.

Section 11.6 Measurements

Slotted Line Metrology & Unknown Load Determination

At gigahertz frequencies, measuring discrete voltages and currents directly is physically impossible. A slotted line (precision coaxial or waveguide section with a longitudinal slot and capacitive probe) measures standing wave profiles directly:

4-Step Algorithm for Extracting Unknown $Z_L$:
  1. Measure VSWR: Connect unknown load $Z_L$. Probe voltage maximum $V_{\max}$ and minimum $V_{\min}$. Calculate $s = V_{\max} / V_{\min}$. Draw the constant $s$-circle on the Smith chart.
  2. Find Wavelength $\lambda$: Distance between two adjacent minima on the line is $\Delta z_{\min} = \lambda / 2 \implies \lambda = 2(z_{\min, 2} - z_{\min, 1})$. Calculate frequency $f = u / \lambda$.
  3. Establish Short-Circuit Reference: Replace load with a short circuit ($Z_L = 0$). Note location of voltage minimum $z_{\text{ref}}$. (Minima are used because sharp dip provides $> 10\times$ higher spatial resolution than broad maxima).
  4. Rotate Toward Load: Calculate shift distance $\ell = (z_{\text{load min}} - z_{\text{ref}})$. Convert to wavelengths: $\ell / \lambda$. Start at $V_{\min}$ on the Smith chart (leftmost point $r = 1/s$) and rotate Toward Load (counter-clockwise) by angle $\theta = (\ell / \lambda) \times 720^\circ$. Read normalized $z_L = r + jx \implies Z_L = Z_0 z_L$.
Section 11.7 Time-Domain

Transients on Transmission Lines & Lattice (Bounce) Diagrams

When a high-speed digital pulse or DC step is launched onto a line, the finite transit time delay $t_1 = \ell / u$ causes the voltage and current to evolve through a discrete sequence of forward and backward wave reflections before settling to steady state.

Key Transient Parameters: $$t_1 = \frac{\ell}{u} = \ell\sqrt{LC} \quad (\text{One-Way Propagation Transit Time})$$ $$V_0 = \frac{Z_0}{Z_g + Z_0}V_g, \qquad I_0 = \frac{V_g}{Z_g + Z_0} = \frac{V_0}{Z_0} \quad (\text{Initial Voltage \& Current at } t = 0^+)$$ $$\Gamma_L = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} \quad (\text{Load Voltage Reflection Coefficient}), \qquad \Gamma_G = \frac{Z_g - Z_0}{Z_g + Z_0} \quad (\text{Generator Voltage Reflection Coefficient})$$ $$\text{Current Reflection Coefficients: } -\Gamma_L \text{ at load, } -\Gamma_G \text{ at generator}$$
Time Interval Generator Voltage $V(0, t)$ Load Voltage $V(\ell, t)$ Physical Wavefront Event
$0 \le t < t_1$ $V_0$ $0$ Incident wave traveling towards load. Load is completely unaware of source.
$t_1 \le t < 2t_1$ $V_0$ $V_0(1 + \Gamma_L)$ Wave hits load at $t=t_1$, generates reflected wave $\Gamma_L V_0$ returning to source.
$2t_1 \le t < 3t_1$ $V_0(1 + \Gamma_L + \Gamma_G \Gamma_L)$ $V_0(1 + \Gamma_L)$ Reflected wave reaches generator at $t=2t_1$, bounces as $\Gamma_G \Gamma_L V_0$.
$3t_1 \le t < 4t_1$ $V_0(1 + \Gamma_L + \Gamma_G \Gamma_L)$ $V_0(1 + \Gamma_L + \Gamma_G \Gamma_L + \Gamma_G \Gamma_L^2)$ Second reflection arrives at load at $t=3t_1$.
$t \to \infty$ (Steady State) $$\frac{Z_L}{Z_L + Z_g}V_g$$ $$\frac{Z_L}{Z_L + Z_g}V_g$$ Infinite geometric series sum: $V_\infty = V_0(1 + \Gamma_L) \sum_{k=0}^\infty (\Gamma_G \Gamma_L)^k$.

Sadiku Example 11.3 ✓ Verified
A lossy transmission line 2 m long operating at $\omega = 10^6\text{ rad/s}$ has $\alpha = 8\text{ dB/m}, \beta = 1\text{ rad/m}, Z_0 = 60 + j40\ \Omega$. Driven by $V_g = 10\angle 0^\circ\text{ V}, Z_g = 40\ \Omega$, terminated in $Z_L = 20 + j50\ \Omega$. Determine: (a) Input impedance $Z_{in}$, (b) Sending-end current $I(0)$, (c) Current at line midpoint $z = \ell/2$.

(a) Convert Attenuation & Compute $Z_{in}$:

$$\alpha = \frac{8\text{ dB/m}}{8.686} = 0.921\text{ Np/m} \implies \gamma = \alpha + j\beta = 0.921 + j1\text{ m}^{-1}$$ $$\gamma \ell = 2(0.921 + j1) = 1.84 + j2 \implies \tanh \gamma \ell = \frac{\sinh(1.84+j2)}{\cosh(1.84+j2)} = 1.033 - j0.03929$$ $$Z_{in} = Z_0 \left[\frac{Z_L + Z_0 \tanh \gamma \ell}{Z_0 + Z_L \tanh \gamma \ell}\right] = (60 + j40)\left[\frac{(20+j50) + (60+j40)(1.033-j0.03929)}{(60+j40) + (20+j50)(1.033-j0.03929)}\right] = \mathbf{60.25 + j38.79\ \Omega}$$

(b) Sending-End Current:

$$I_0 = \frac{V_g}{Z_g + Z_{in}} = \frac{10}{40 + (60.25 + j38.79)} = \frac{10}{100.25 + j38.79} = \mathbf{93.03\angle -21.15^\circ\text{ mA}}$$

(c) Midpoint Current $z = \ell/2 = 1\text{ m}$:

$$V_0 = Z_{in} I_0 = (71.66\angle 32.77^\circ)(0.09303\angle -21.15^\circ) = 6.667\angle 11.62^\circ\text{ V}$$ $$V_0^+ = \frac{1}{2}(V_0 + Z_0 I_0) = 6.687\angle 12.08^\circ\text{ V}, \qquad V_0^- = \frac{1}{2}(V_0 - Z_0 I_0) = 0.0518\angle 260^\circ\text{ V}$$ $$\text{At } z = 1\text{ m}, \gamma z = 0.921 + j1\text{ rad} = 0.921 + j57.3^\circ:$$ $$I_s(1) = \frac{V_0^+}{Z_0}e^{-\gamma z} - \frac{V_0^-}{Z_0}e^{\gamma z} = \mathbf{35.10\angle 281^\circ\text{ mA} = 6.673 - j34.456\text{ mA}}$$
Sadiku Example 11.4 ✓ Verified
A lossless $50\ \Omega$ line of length $30\text{ m}$ operates at $2\text{ MHz}$ with $u = 0.6c$, terminated by $Z_L = 60 + j40\ \Omega$. Find: (a) $\Gamma$, (b) $s$, (c) $Z_{in}$. Solve analytically and verify via Smith chart.

Analytical Solution:

$$\Gamma = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} = \frac{60+j40-50}{60+j40+50} = \frac{10+j40}{110+j40} = \mathbf{0.3523\angle 56.0^\circ}$$ $$s = \frac{1 + |\Gamma|}{1 - |\Gamma|} = \frac{1 + 0.3523}{1 - 0.3523} = \mathbf{2.088}$$ $$\beta = \frac{\omega}{u} = \frac{2\pi \times 2\times 10^6}{0.6(3\times 10^8)} = \frac{4\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{45}\text{ rad/m} \implies \beta \ell = \frac{\pi}{45}(30) = \frac{2\pi}{3} = 120^\circ$$ $$Z_{in} = 50 \left[\frac{(60+j40) + j50\tan 120^\circ}{50 + j(60+j40)\tan 120^\circ}\right] = 50\left[\frac{60 + j40 - j86.60}{50 - j86.60(0.6+j0.4)}\right] = \mathbf{23.97 + j1.35\ \Omega = 24.01\angle 3.22^\circ\ \Omega}$$

Smith Chart Verification:

$$z_L = \frac{60+j40}{50} = 1.2 + j0.8 \implies \text{Plot point } P(1.2, 0.8)$$ $$\text{Measure } |\Gamma| = \frac{OP}{OQ} = \frac{3.2\text{ cm}}{9.1\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{0.3516}, \quad \theta_\Gamma = \mathbf{56^\circ}$$ $$\text{Rotate } \beta \ell = 120^\circ \implies \text{Smith Chart angular shift } 2\times 120^\circ = 240^\circ \text{ CW toward generator: } z_{in} \approx 0.47 + j0.03$$ $$Z_{in} = 50(0.47 + j0.03) = \mathbf{23.5 + j1.5\ \Omega} \quad (\text{Close alignment with analytical!})$$

Exam Mastery

Critical Academic Traps & Examination Checklist

⚠️ Trap 1: Confusing Line Resistance $R$ ($\Omega/\text{m}$) with $R_0 = \text{Re}(Z_0)$ ($\Omega$)
$R$ is the per-unit-length series resistance of the wire in $\Omega/\text{m}$, which dissipates ohmic heat. $R_0$ is the real part of the characteristic impedance $Z_0$ in ohms ($\Omega$), representing the ratio of traveling voltage to traveling current. For a lossless line, $R = 0$, but $R_0 = \sqrt{L/C} \ne 0$!
⚠️ Trap 2: Angular Multiplication on the Smith Chart ($2\times$)
Moving a physical distance $\Delta z$ on a transmission line changes the phase of the reflection coefficient by $2\beta \Delta z$. Therefore, an electrical line length of $30^\circ$ corresponds to a $60^\circ$ mechanical rotation on the Smith chart! Always multiply electrical distance in degrees by 2 when plotting.
⚠️ Trap 3: Transient Current Signs vs Voltage Signs
Voltage reflection coefficients at load and generator are $\Gamma_L$ and $\Gamma_G$. The corresponding current reflection coefficients are $-\Gamma_L$ and $-\Gamma_G$. Forgetting the negative sign flips the current staircase and fails the steady-state boundary check $I_\infty = V_\infty / Z_L$.
