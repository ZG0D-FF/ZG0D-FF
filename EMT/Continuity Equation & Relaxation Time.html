Continuity Equation & Relaxation Time

Electric Fields in Material Space • Exam Revision Notes

1. Principle of Charge Conservation

Due to the principle of charge conservation, the time rate of decrease of charge within a given volume must be equal to the net outward current flow through the closed surface of the volume.

Current coming out of a closed surface is:

∮ J · dS = − dQin/dt

where

  • J = Current density
  • Qin = Total charge enclosed within the closed surface

2. Derivation of the Continuity Equation

The enclosed charge is expressed as:

Q = ∫V ρv dV

Substituting into the charge conservation equation:

∮ J · dS = − d/dt ∫V ρv dV

Using the Divergence Theorem:

∫V (∇ · J) dV = − ∫V ∂ρv/∂t dV

Since both integrals are over the same arbitrary volume:

∇ · J = − ∂ρv/∂t
This is the Continuity Equation.

3. Physical Meaning

The continuity equation states that:

  • If current leaves a region, the charge stored inside decreases.
  • If current enters a region, the stored charge increases.
  • Charge can neither be created nor destroyed.

4. Relaxation Time

Inside a conducting material, use:

Ohm's Law

J = σE

Gauss's Law

∇ · E = ρv/ε

Substitute both into the continuity equation:

∂ρv/∂t + (σ/ε)ρv = 0

This is a homogeneous linear ordinary differential equation.

5. Solution of the Differential Equation

Separate variables:

dρv/ρv = − (σ/ε) dt

Integrating both sides:

ln ρv = − (σ/ε)t + C

Therefore, the charge density becomes:

ρv(t)=ρ0e−σt/ε

where

  • ρ0 = Initial charge density
  • σ = Conductivity
  • ε = Permittivity

6. Relaxation Time Constant

The relaxation time is:

τ = ε/σ

Hence, the charge density can also be written as:

ρv(t)=ρ0e−t/τ
The relaxation time represents how quickly excess charge disappears inside a material.

10. Two-Mark Exam Points

State the Continuity Equation

The continuity equation is

∇·J = − ∂ρv/∂t

It expresses the conservation of electric charge.

Define Relaxation Time

Relaxation time is the time required for excess charge inside a material to decay exponentially.

τ = ε/σ

Why is relaxation time very small for conductors?

Conductors have very high conductivity, so

τ = ε/σ

becomes extremely small, causing charge to move rapidly to the surface.
